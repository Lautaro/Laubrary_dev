## Paste Insert (2026-10-13, T-0594)

Paste Insert sits beside Paste in the Pattern tool column (rows-plus icon) and on Ctrl+Shift+V, next to Ctrl+C / Ctrl+X / Ctrl+V. It works on the columns the clipboard lands on: from the cursor row down, those columns move down by the clipboard's height, rows pushed past the end are dropped and counted, then the clipboard is pasted into the gap; one Undo step. Moved values are read from a copy taken before any write, because a command's identifier and its value are two fields over one cell (the first implementation lost the moved values exactly there, caught by the check). Automation lanes belong to a track rather than a column, so a track's automation points move with it only when the paste covers every visible column of that track; a paste into some of a track's columns leaves its automation in place. The dropped count shows on the window's status line ("⚠ Paste Insert dropped 2 rows pushed past the pattern end."), and also counts dropped automation points and copied rows that did not fit; it clears at the next cursor move. Hidden sub-columns (for example a pan value whose column is not shown) do not move, matching what Copy and Paste already do with hidden fields.

## FX columns: count, +N expander and stacked commands (2026-10-13, T-0597)

## Glide time, legato slide and legato velocity (2026-10-13, T-0627 and T-0628)

Measured before changing anything, from rendered audio (instantaneous pitch from the zero crossings of a pure tone; a note at row 0, the next at row 8, glide from the previous note): a 100 ms glide reached 50% of the pitch distance at 58–98 ms, 63% at 84–133 ms, 95% at 270–339 ms and 99% at 430–505 ms on the synth and FM alike (upwards and downwards differ because the slide was not even in pitch); 50 ms and 500 ms scaled the same way. Cause: the glide approached the new pitch exponentially with Glide ms as its time constant (and in frequency ratio, not in semitones), so the stated time was only the time to cover about two thirds of the way. Defined now: Glide ms is the time the slide takes to arrive; the pitch moves evenly in semitones, so 50% is reached at half the time and 100% at the stated time. Glide ms also governs Legato: an overlapping note slides from the pitch the held note has reached to the new note over Glide ms (0 jumps); Both does that and also slides a detached note from the previous one. A volume command takes over a slide at once.

## Instrument envelopes: loops on time, rows, playheads, loop and sustain markers (2026-10-13, T-0629)

Reproduced before fixing, from rendered audio (a 200 ms looping ramp held for 20 s, timing each loop restart from the per-cycle level or pulse duty): a looped volume envelope (the generic per-note envelope) restarted on time; a looped pulse-width envelope (one of the synth's own five: Blend, pulse width, B ratio, PM, detune) restarted 96 ms late after 20 s, half a loop. Cause: those five envelopes kept time with a single-precision clock that added one sample's duration per sample; once a note had been held a few seconds the rounding of each addition made the clock run up to 0.7% fast or slow, so loops (and everything else on those envelopes) drifted. Fixed with a double-precision clock. Also measured and left as is: the synth's own envelopes are smoothed over 5 ms so a value change cannot click, which rounds a loop's sharp restart (a 0.1 → 0.9 pulse-width ramp bottoms out at about 0.17).

New: an envelope can be measured in song rows (Time in › Rows), so its length, points, loop and sustain follow the song's tempo; switching keeps its length at the current tempo. The renderer writes, once per rendered slice, a fixed record per voice with its position on each envelope (no allocation and no managed work on the audio side); the editor reads the song renderer's and the instrument preview's records about 30 times a second and draws one playhead per sounding note (unison voices of one note count once; released notes are drawn fainter). The curve shows the loop as a shaded band between two markers with a forward (←) or ping-pong (↔) arrow, and the sustain point as a dashed S marker; both are dragged on the curve (rows snap to whole rows), one Undo step per drag. The time axis is labelled in milliseconds/seconds or rows, both ends always shown. Axis legends (time along the bottom, the value turned up the side) are switched in the curve's context menu (right-click its background), a per-user preference. The Loop % and Sustain % sliders were removed; the markers replace them. Reusable parts went into ZUI (see the Laubrary CHANGELOG): loop and sustain markers, several playheads, tick labels, switchable axis captions, a background context hook and a ZuiValueControl decorate hook.

## Blend envelope section (2026-10-13, T-0630)

Question: is the separate Blend envelope (a fixed attack-decay-sustain-release for Blend) redundant now that the Blend slider can be an envelope? Measured with `CheckBlendAdsr` 3/0, sine A against square B mixed at the same pitch (A 50 ms, D 200 ms, S 40%, R 300 ms), comparing the ADSR with the same shape drawn as Blend's own envelope (points at the peak and the decay end, the sustain marker there, a release segment): held into its sustain they differ by 2.5% of the signal during attack and decay (the envelope's 5 ms smoothing) and 0.02% while held; after note-off by 13% (the ADSR returns to the Blend slider's value once its release ends, the envelope stays at its last point); a note released during the decay differs by 8.3% (the ADSR releases from where it is, the envelope plays on through its shape). While the ADSR is on, Blend 0% and 100% differ by only 0.24%: the ADSR, not the slider, decides Blend, which is two controls fighting over one value.

Decision: redundant for making sounds, but not convertible without changing older sounds. So the section is no longer offered; a sound saved with it switched on still shows it, titled Older blend ADSR, and plays exactly as before (the engine path is unchanged; rendered before/after bit-identical); switching it off keeps its stages and Undo brings it back; once off it is gone from the page. The same applies to a sampler zone's second-sample blend ADSR. New sounds shape Blend with the Blend slider's own envelope (right-click Blend › Envelope), which has the loop, rows and playheads.

Verified by probe: `CheckBlendAdsr` 3/0 (above); updated `CheckInstrumentOverhaulWorkflow` (a new sound shows no older ADSR; an older one does; switching it off keeps its stages and cannot unfold) and `CheckInstrumentReworkWorkflow` (a zone without it shows none).
