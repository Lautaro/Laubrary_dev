# T-0098 — Investigator C: 3D Shaper vs Pyre

Research only. No code changed in either project. No Unity/Coplay used.

---

## 1. What is 3D Shaper, concretely?

**Plain answer.** 3D Shaper is a standalone local web app (Python `server.py` + a single-file `public/index.html` front end) for building stylised "2.5D" pixel-art models by hand: you draw a 2D silhouette (a **shape**), give it a **material** (colour + surface behaviour), stack several shape+material **layers** into a **model**, give each layer depth (**extrusion**) and an edge treatment, and optionally animate parameters on a per-model timeline. It renders live in the browser as you edit; there is no 3D mesh, camera, or physically-based renderer — it's a stylised per-pixel rasteriser (README.md "Using the app" § Vocabulary; PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md § Renderer and interaction constraints: "Rendering is a stylised 2.5D composition, not a claim of physically accurate 3D geometry").

**How 3D → pixel art happens.** The renderer (`renderModelGrid()`, confirmed at `public/index.html:1392` onward) is a per-cell signed-distance-field rasteriser: each model is flattened onto a height-map grid with a z-buffer (`cellMaterial`/`belowMaterial`/`cellLayer` arrays, `public/index.html:1392`), one winning material per cell, then shaded per pixel with a Lambert+Fresnel-style lighting pass (`normalX`/`normalY`/`normalZ` computed from neighbouring heights, `public/index.html:1491-1495`; Fresnel term at `:1510`). There is no camera/projection choice — it's a fixed top-down/orthographic-style composite, not a user-adjustable 3D camera. Resolution is a per-model "Render resolution" (grid cell count), animatable (GAP_ANALYSIS.md § Canvas and UI; § Animation: canvas Width/Height/Render resolution added T-0024). Palette is per-material primary/secondary colour plus 11 named 2D patterns (`PATTERN_TYPES`, `project_document.py:18`) — not a fixed global palette or dithering; there is no dithering feature at all (not mentioned anywhere in the docs or `PATTERN_TYPES`). Outlines exist as a per-layer edge/border feature (pixel-material edge ring, 2–128 cells, full 360° orientation, reach/position — GAP_ANALYSIS.md § Pixel edges) and a separate silhouette outline/border for occlusion (GAP_ANALYSIS.md § Layer border).

**Primitives.** Eight built-in 2D primitives (rectangle, ellipse, diamond, triangle, hexagon, octagon, capsule, star — README.md § Choosing a shape; GAP_ANALYSIS.md § 2D shapes: "Better than asked"), each with width/height/skew/corner-rounding/pinch-bulge controls, extruded with 7 extrusion profiles and 6 bevel profiles (BUILD_PLAN.md T-0009/GAP_ANALYSIS.md § 3D modeller). There are no true 3D primitives (no sphere/cylinder/box mesh) — depth is a 2D silhouette swept/extruded, not a 3D solid.

**Finished vs gap (per GAP_ANALYSIS.md, the authoritative honest list, "per docs, unconfirmed" not needed here since it is itself an audit doc).** Roughly 33 of ~35 originally requested capabilities are "Implemented"; 2 are "Partial" (a copied complex shape still showed ~10 dead reshaping sliders — fixed under the audit task; the plasma-pattern swatch preview was frozen — also fixed). Two later investigation tasks (T-0014 Kiln-porting, T-0015 transparency/gradient) are explicitly **not implemented**, filed as research only. Materials have **no opacity/alpha field at all** (confirmed in `normalise_material()`, `project_document.py:343-347` — fields are `primaryColor, secondaryColor, surface, reflection, emission, pattern, plasma`, no alpha) — this is the single biggest rendering gap per TRANSPARENCY_GRADIENT_INVESTIGATION.md, which is a plan, not built work.

**Is it animated over time, or single-image only?** It IS animated, but only as a **live in-app preview/scrub**, not an exported asset. Every model owns a `timeline: {frameCount, fps, envelopes}` (`public/index.html:2409`; schema `project_document.py:413`), and 49 parameters across layers/materials/lights/global-lighting/canvas are keyframe-animatable with linear/hold/smooth interpolation (GAP_ANALYSIS.md § Animation; README.md § Animating a parameter). The renderer re-evaluates every enabled envelope per frame and redraws (BUILD_PLAN.md T-0011). **However: there is no export path of any kind.** Grepping the whole app for export/spritesheet/PNG-download/toDataURL turns up nothing except the unrelated reference-image upload feature (`public/index.html:3882`, `server.py:69`), and PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md's own "Intentional v1 limits" explicitly lists "no ... video export" and no import/export interchange formats at all. So today, 3D Shaper can show you an animation happening live in a browser tab, but there is no mechanism to get a sequence of frames (PNG sequence, sprite sheet, or otherwise) out of it as a file. **This is the single most important fact for the Pyre integration question**, since a Pyre generator's contract is "produce frames the runtime can play," and Shaper currently cannot hand any frames to anything.

## 2. Its LAYER system

**Plain answer.** A Shaper "layer" is a step in a **2D silhouette boolean composition** — it has no colour, no depth, no lifetime; it is purely "which shape, in what mode, added to the accumulation so far." This is a fundamentally different concept from a **Pyre layer**, which is a full timeline citizen (its own particle/swarm system, matte role, lifetime window, modifier stack) that also happens to have colour and depth. Conflating the two words is the single easiest way to misdesign the integration.

**Precisely, in Shaper:** Per PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md's Phase 2 addendum (§ Vocabulary correction), there is now only **one** shape type: every shape (library asset or a model layer's private snapshot) is a stack of one-or-more **layers**, each layer being a chosen primitive-or-shape plus a **fuse mode**. The four fuse modes, confirmed both in the schema (`Complex-shape component layer-mode registry`, PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md) and live in the running app via the T-0058 screenshot (`D:\CODEZ\AgentHQ\3D Shaper\T-0058_layer-fusion-radios.png`) and its task file (`D:\CODEZ\AgentHQ\3D Shaper\.agenthq\tasks\T-0058.md`), are: **Add** (union), **Add soft** (union blended by a viscosity strength+sharpness pair — genuinely two independent knobs per GAP_ANALYSIS.md § Complex shapes), **Subtract** (true boolean hole, only possible because the renderer is per-cell rather than a convex hull), **Subtract soft** (soft-edged cut, viscosity 0 = no cut, viscosity 1 = identical to hard Subtract). Order is significant and stable (stored order = evaluation order); a layer can itself be another whole library shape's frozen snapshot, nested up to 4 levels / 512 total pieces (README.md § Building a shape out of other shapes). **A shape layer carries no colour and no depth of its own** — colour/material and extrusion/depth are only ever applied once, at the **model** layer above the shape (a *model* layer wraps a shape snapshot with position/transform/material/extrusion — PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md's Model/Layer document types table). So "layer" is overloaded on purpose in Shaper's own vocabulary: a **model layer** owns colour+depth+lifetime-in-the-model-stack; a **shape layer** (inside that model layer's shape, or inside a reusable library shape) owns only silhouette combination.

**Precisely, in Pyre:** a `PyreLayer` (`Assets\Packages\Laubrary\Runtime\Pyre\Pyre.cs:180`) is a single timeline entity that already bundles: a lifetime window (`startFrame`/`endFrame`, ported 1:1 from Pyre1, `Pyre.cs:186-195`), a matte role (Draw / WriteMatte / LumaMatte with channel/combine/clip/invert/heightmap fields, `Pyre.cs:198-250`), an optional swarm (a whole per-particle sub-system: count, spawn mode, chaos, timing, per-particle life fraction, orientation, whole-cloud turn/tilt/roll/scale, `Pyre.cs:660-756`), and an ordered `modifiers` list of `PyreModifier` (geometry/pixel/post effects with their own frame-persistent state, `Pyre.cs:756-773`). Colour (via a Fill/Ramp), shape (via `shapeForm`), depth-look (3D solids get real per-face lighting, see §4) all live on this same one layer. There is **no separate "shape stack that composes before colour is applied"** concept in Pyre at all — a Pyre layer IS the shape-plus-everything-else in one object, drawn per particle per frame (`DrawFacetSolid`, `PyreRenderer.cs:4152` onward, called per particle every frame via the `own`/`life`/`frameIndex` parameters).

**The exact difference, stated plainly.** A Shaper *shape* layer is one step of a pure, colourless, timeless 2D boolean stack (its only job: contribute to or cut a silhouette). A Pyre layer is a colour-having, depth-having, time-having, particle-having, matte-having, modifier-having full render citizen. **They are not the same kind of thing and do not compose the same way** — Shaper layers combine via CSG-like add/subtract/soft-fuse into ONE silhouette that is then coloured once; Pyre layers combine by being drawn/composited one after another (optionally gated by mattes) onto one frame, each already fully coloured. Any integration must not name-collide these two "layer" concepts, or the design will silently conflate "compose a shape" with "compose a scene."

## 3. Its MATERIAL system

**Plain answer.** A Shaper material today is a shared, named bag of *look* parameters applied by reference to any layer using it (edit once, every user updates — README.md § Materials view); it is "crude" in the sense that it has no real depth-of-transparency, no true spatial gradients, and no procedural-over-time richness beyond a hand-rolled plasma churn and pattern set — it is a fixed enum of 7 surface *categories* with hardcoded shading branches, not an open/composable material graph.

**What data it carries (schema, confirmed).** `normalise_material()` (`project_document.py:343-347`): `id`, `name`, `primaryColor`, `secondaryColor`, `surface` (one of 7 `SURFACE_CATEGORIES`: matte/glossy/metallic/glass/gelatinous/plasma/emissive — `project_document.py:17`), `reflection` (0-1), `emission` (`strength` 0-100, `radius` 0-8192), `pattern` (`type` from 12 `PATTERN_TYPES`, `scale`, `angle`, `seed` — `project_document.py:18`), `plasma` (a churn amount). No opacity/alpha field exists (confirmed above, §1).

**How it's applied.** A model layer references a `materialId`; the renderer resolves the surface-category branch per pixel using the per-cell normal/Fresnel already computed for the height-map (glass blends toward whatever single material sits directly below it in the z-buffer — `belowMaterial`, `public/index.html:1516-1517` — a one-layer lookup, not real stacked alpha compositing per TRANSPARENCY_GRADIENT_INVESTIGATION.md); gelatinous adds wrap-around lighting and a moving surface wobble (`public/index.html:1493`, driven by `clock`); plasma and emissive pulse/churn per frame independent of keyframes (GAP_ANALYSIS.md § Animation). Patterns blend primary/secondary colour in the layer's own local shape space so they stay glued to the silhouette as it moves (`patternMix()`, `public/index.html:1305-1310`).

**Planned future (per docs, unconfirmed — these are investigation docs, nothing built).** TRANSPARENCY_GRADIENT_INVESTIGATION.md's top recommendation is a real per-material `opacity` field with genuine back-to-front alpha compositing through the z-buffer stack (today only a 2-layer approximation is reachable without restructuring the rasteriser's inner loop, `public/index.html:1204-1240` region). KILN_PORTING_INVESTIGATION.md recommends porting two families of Kiln-grown Python art algorithms (shape-agnostic, fed only a coverage mask + distance-from-edge map) into the material/edge system: "Pyre Fill"/"Pyre Fill Dissolve" (decorative interior materials: engraved metal, leaded glass, sunburst fabric, brass, fire, sand-pour/ink-wash dissolve) as new material types, and "Pyre Edge"/"Pyre Edge Dissolve" (bevel/glow/rim-light/burning-border looks) as new edge treatments, plus a reusable "Pyre Wipe" animated mask usable with either. **Both are investigations only — zero implementation exists for either.**

## 4. Overlap with Pyre

**Plain answer, fair and concrete.** They overlap where both do "give a flat 2D silhouette a fake sense of depth and light," but they arrived by opposite routes: Shaper composes an arbitrary silhouette via 2D boolean ops then infers a height-map and lights it; Pyre's 3D-ish forms (Box/Pyramid/Can/Gem/Orb/Ring) are literal 3D geometry (real vertices/faces or analytic sphere/annulus maths) rotated in 3D and lit with real per-face Blinn-Phong, but each is a single fixed primitive — none of them compose with each other into an arbitrary silhouette the way Shaper's layers do.

- **Where Shaper is better:** arbitrary, composable 2D silhouettes via 4 fuse modes with real CSG holes and soft blending (GAP_ANALYSIS.md § Complex shapes; confirmed schema in PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md's layer-mode registry) — Pyre's `ShapeForm` has no equivalent at all; each Pyre 3D solid is one hardcoded primitive (`BuildBoxGeometry`/`BuildPyramidGeometry`/`BuildCanGeometry`, `PyreRenderer.cs:4182-4184`) with no way to subtract one from another or fuse two solids' silhouettes. Shaper's occlusion-aware outline (a buried layer's border painted onto whatever covers it, GAP_ANALYSIS.md § Layer border) is also a capability Pyre's 3D forms do not have — Pyre's `DrawFacetSolid` draws hard edge lines per visible face of ONE solid (`PyreRenderer.cs:4152` onward) but has no concept of one whole shape's silhouette occluding another's outline.
- **Where Pyre is better:** true per-particle 3D transforms — real yaw/tilt/roll composed as actual 3D rotation matrices applied to real vertex data (`Rot()` closure, `PyreRenderer.cs:4193-4198`), genuine backface culling via face-normal dot-product (`PyreRenderer.cs:4207-4211`), and everything is driven by the *existing* Pyre particle/swarm/lifetime/modifier machinery for free — a Pyre 3D solid is already a first-class citizen of a timed, swarming, matte-able, modifier-stackable render pipeline (`Pyre.cs:180-773`). Shaper's model layers have transform/position but no swarm concept, no matte/clip concept, and (per §1) no export path at all — Shaper's animation lives and dies inside its own browser tab.
- **Where they are straight-up duplicates that should not both exist long-term:** the "fake a lit, extruded, stylised solid from a 2D-ish description" problem itself. Shaper's SDF-heightmap-normal-Fresnel pipeline (`public/index.html:1392-1517`) and Pyre's facet-solid Blinn-Phong pipeline (`PyreRenderer.cs:4152` onward) are two independently-built answers to a closely related question (make a flat pixel canvas read as lit 3D). Maintaining both long-term, especially once/if Shaper's silhouette-composition richness gets ported into Pyre (see §5/§6), would be redundant lighting math nobody asked for twice.

## 5. Integration options

**Recommendation up front:** given today's actual state (Shaper has no export path at all — §1 — and its "layer" system is a shape-composition concept Pyre has never had), the only option that is buildable **today** without first building brand-new infrastructure in one project or the other is treating **3D Shaper as an external authoring tool that exports an asset Pyre consumes** — and even that requires building the export feature first, since it does not exist. The nested-sub-editor and flat-1:1-mapping options both additionally assume Shaper becomes a *live, per-frame* Pyre generator, which is a much bigger, ongoing commitment (Shaper's renderer would need to run inside Unity, or Pyre would need to re-implement Shaper's SDF rasteriser in C#) than "port a finished picture/sequence over." Recommendation: **start with export-to-asset (Option C below); revisit nested-sub-editor only if Shaper's silhouette-composition richness turns out to be something Pyre users want to keep editing live, per-project, inside Unity** — that is a real UX want, but it is a second, separable decision from "can Pyre play a Shaper-made shape at all."

**Option A — Nested sub-editor.** One Pyre layer holds a reference to a whole Shaper *model* (its own shape stack, materials, lighting, animation), and Pyre's `PyreWindow` opens a genuinely embedded Shaper-like sub-editor when you double-click that layer, with its own shape-layer list, fuse-mode radios, material picker, etc. — a save on that sub-editor updates the one Pyre layer's baked output.
- *UX:* closest to "Shaper inside Pyre" — a user builds the whole composed model without leaving the Pyre window, at the cost of a second, differently-shaped UI living inside one PyreWindow tab (Pyre's own layer list ≠ Shaper's shape-layer list; they'd sit at two different nesting depths simultaneously).
- *Cost:* highest. Either re-host Shaper's actual JS/canvas renderer inside a Unity `WebView`/`UIElements` surface (heavy, fragile, and defeats "local-first standalone app" per README.md's own framing) or reimplement Shaper's per-cell SDF rasteriser, material surfaces, and fuse-mode compositor natively in C# — essentially a full port of `public/index.html`'s rendering core (`renderModelGrid`, `public/index.html:1392` onward) plus its shape/material data model (`project_document.py`).
- *Interaction with Pyre's own machinery:* a nested sub-shape has **no natural way to inherit** Pyre's swarm, matte role, or per-modifier stack, because those are concepts that live on the *outer* Pyre layer, one level above where the Shaper content lives — the outer Pyre layer could still carry swarm/matte/modifiers around the *whole baked Shaper output* (treating the nested editor's result as one flat image/sequence, same as any other `ShapeForm`), but the *individual* Shaper shape-layers inside it could never each get their own swarm or matte, only the composed whole could. This is the same shape-vs-scene layering mismatch flagged in §2.

**Option B — Flat 1:1 mapping (one Pyre layer ↔ one Shaper shape-layer).**
- *UX:* a user builds a shape in Shaper's Shapes tab as today, then in Pyre each of that shape's own layers (its fuse-mode stack) becomes a separate Pyre layer, with Pyre's own matte-role field pressed into doing the fuse-mode's job (e.g. "Subtract" ≈ a `WriteMatte` layer whose coverage clips the layer(s) below it, inverted).
- *Cost:* medium, but semantically leaky: Shaper's 4 fuse modes are silhouette booleans evaluated bottom-to-top into ONE outline before any colour exists (§2); Pyre's matte system clips *already-drawn, already-coloured* layers via alpha/luma channels (`Pyre.cs:198-233`). Soft-add/soft-subtract's viscosity blend (a genuinely continuous silhouette blend, not a hard clip) has **no matching primitive in Pyre's matte model at all** — it would need new renderer code regardless of the mapping choice, so "flat 1:1" does not actually avoid porting Shaper's fusion maths into Pyre, it just avoids porting the *UI* for authoring it.
- *Interaction with Pyre machinery:* this option is the one most naturally compatible with swarms/mattes/modifiers, since each mapped Pyre layer is a normal `PyreLayer` and inherits everything a layer already can do — but that is also its problem: a shape's 5 fuse-mode components becoming 5 independent swarm-capable, matte-capable, modifier-stackable Pyre layers is far more render/authoring surface than "one shape" conceptually is, and none of that per-component richness is something Shaper shapes ever needed or exposed.

**Option C — Shaper as an external authoring tool that exports an asset Pyre consumes.**
- *UX:* a user builds and iterates in Shaper's own browser UI exactly as today (nothing about Shaper's authoring experience changes), then exports either a static baked texture/normal-ish data or (once Shaper's timeline is wired to an export) a frame sequence; a Pyre `ShapeForm` (or a new one) loads that asset the same way `Sprite`/`Playback3D` already load external content today (`Pyre.cs:99-116` enum; `ShapeForm.Sprite` reads a texture, `PyreRenderer.cs:203`).
- *Cost:* requires building Shaper's export feature first (confirmed nonexistent, §1) — likely a per-frame render-to-PNG-sequence or sprite-sheet dump driven by the existing `frameCount`/`fps`/envelope evaluation the renderer already does live (`public/index.html:1392` is already called once per displayed frame; wrapping it in a headless per-frame capture loop is a bounded, scoped addition, not a rewrite). On the Pyre side, cost is low: it is exactly the shape of problem `ShapeForm.Sprite`/`Playback3D` already solve (load external frames, play them on the timeline) — see §1's note that Playback3D itself is still editor-preview-only with **no runtime bake** (`Pyre.cs:609-621`; `PyreRenderer.cs:262-269` is a documented stub), which is itself a cautionary precedent: Pyre has already built one "external 3D content" `ShapeForm` and left its runtime path unfinished.
- *Interaction with Pyre machinery:* best fit of the three. The imported asset is just frames a normal `PyreLayer` plays, so it gets swarm/matte/lifetime-window/modifiers for free and for real, exactly as `Sprite` does today — no leaky semantic mapping (Options A/B both hit the shape-vs-scene mismatch; this one sidesteps it entirely by treating Shaper's finished output as a texture, the same category of thing a Sprite layer already is).

**Option D — Port the shape maths into Pyre and drop the web app.**
- *UX:* everything happens inside Unity/Pyre; no second app, no export step, one tool.
- *Cost:* highest ongoing cost of upkeep even if the initial port is scoped down (need at minimum: the per-cell SDF/height-map rasteriser, the 4-mode silhouette fusion with viscosity, the 7 surface-category shading branches, the 12 patterns) — and it throws away Shaper's still-active iteration (T-0058 shipped 2026-08-30, the day this investigation runs) and its independent product-contract/acceptance-criteria discipline (PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md), which is a real, working process the owner would be discarding, not merely code.
- *Recommendation stance:* not recommended now — it forecloses Option A/B later while Shaper is still actively gaining capability (materials/edges investigations queued, GAP_ANALYSIS.md § Queued follow-up work), and it is strictly more work than Option C for a benefit (one tool instead of two) that is nice-to-have, not need-to-have.

## 6. The shape-vs-material split

**Plain answer.** Shaper's docs already draw exactly the shape/material line the owner wants ("a shape is a silhouette... it has no colour of its own. A material is a colour and how it behaves" — README.md § Vocabulary) — but this is a **static** split: Shaper's shape stack has no time dimension of its own (a shape has no keyframes; only a *model layer* wrapping it, or a *material*, is animatable — PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md's Animation parameter registry lists layer transforms/dimensions and material fields as animatable, not raw shape-library geometry). So Shaper is halfway to the owner's vision (shape ≠ material as data), but not the whole way (neither half is required to independently animate over time as a first-class idea — a shape *can* be animated only by way of the model layer that hosts it, and a material *can* churn/pulse only via its own fixed plasma/emissive branches, not an arbitrary generator).

**Does Pyre's current architecture allow shape and material to be two independently-animating passes?** Not today, and this is a real, unverified-by-any-existing-code gap. Every `ShapeForm`'s draw function computes silhouette AND colour together in one pass: `DrawFacetSolid` picks colour via the layer's Fill/Ramp at the same per-pixel point it computes the Lambert/Fresnel lighting term (`PyreRenderer.cs:4152` onward — there's no separate "resolve geometry" then "resolve surface" stage a second, independent generator could plug into). `OrbForm.Render` is even more fused: its whole visual **is** an additive noise field read through a colour ramp (`OrbForm.cs:490` `Render()`; the class doc block states plainly "Colour comes from the variant's Ramp, NOT the layer Fill" — geometry and colour are the literal same field, by design, not two passes that happen to run together).

**Does `PyreShade.cs` already compute anything material-like (normals/depth/lighting) a surface-material pass could sample?** Partially, and it's important to be precise about what it is and isn't. `PyreShade.cs` (`Assets\Packages\Laubrary\Runtime\Pyre\PyreShade.cs`) is **not** a normal/depth/lighting module — despite the name, it is a colour-ramp/LUT/tone-mapping utility library: linear-light gradient evaluation (`EvalStops`, `PyreShade.cs:138`), banded/cel quantisation (`Banded`, `:230/:238`), dual-ramp crossfade (`DualRamp`, `:266`), and additive-emissive tone mapping (`AdditiveEmissive`, `:300/:317`). **The actual normal/depth/lighting math that a surface-material pass would want to sample lives in `PyreRenderer.cs`, not `PyreShade.cs`**: per-face flat normals + backface culling for the 3D solids (`PyreRenderer.cs:4207-4211`), and analytic per-pixel normal + Blinn-Phong point-light + Fresnel for Orb/Ring (documented at `PyreRenderer.cs:2857-2865`, "the ANALYTIC true-3D forms... reuse the SAME per-pixel point-light + Blinn-Phong lighting" as the facet solids). So the raw ingredients a surface-material generator would need (a normal, a light-relative angle, a depth/height value) already exist per-pixel inside Pyre's renderer for its 3D-ish forms specifically — they are just not exposed as a sampleable intermediate result today; they are computed and consumed inline in the same function that also decides final colour.

**What would have to be true for the owner's vision (a shape generates over time; its surface material also generates over time, sampling the shape's geometry) to actually work in Pyre:** (1) a `ShapeForm`'s render path would need to be split into two real stages — "resolve this frame's geometry/coverage/normal/depth per pixel" and "resolve this frame's surface colour per pixel," with the first stage's outputs (coverage mask, normal, depth/height, at minimum) captured into an addressable per-pixel buffer rather than consumed inline, mirroring the coverage-mask + distance-map convention Kiln's own border/fill algorithms already use as their two generic inputs (KILN_PORTING_INVESTIGATION.md § "What Kiln actually is" / § "The relevant projects" — an existing, independently-arrived-at precedent for exactly this split, worth reading as evidence the split is a workable seam, not merely a hopeful one); (2) a second, independently-animating "material generator" stage (analogous to a `PyreForm` in its own right, or a new sibling concept) would need a defined per-pixel input contract (what geometry data it's allowed to sample) and its own clock/life parameter separate from the shape's; (3) Pyre's `PyreLayer` model (§2's `Pyre.cs:180` object) would need to decide whether "shape" and "material" become two fields on one layer (closest to today's single-layer-does-everything shape) or two linked layers (closer to Shaper's own model-layer-wraps-a-shape-snapshot structure, PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md's Layer/Model document types) — this is genuinely a design decision, not something inferable from what exists today in either codebase, and is flagged as UNVERIFIED/open below rather than guessed at.

## UNVERIFIED / open questions

- **Whether Shaper's per-cell rasteriser (`renderModelGrid`) could be wrapped in a headless per-frame capture loop without touching its live-editing code path** — plausible from its structure (it's already called once per displayed frame) but not measured or prototyped; this is the load-bearing assumption behind Option C's "low cost on the Shaper side" claim.
- **Performance of Shaper's SDF rasteriser at Pyre's typical frame budget** (Pyre generators run inside a real-time Unity particle/swarm pipeline; Shaper's renderer safeguard already exists specifically because it gets expensive past ~4M on-screen pixels / 128 visible layers, README.md § Renderer safeguard) — UNVERIFIED whether a Shaper-authored model, exported per-frame, would be cheap enough to bake at typical Pyre canvas sizes (Pyre commonly runs at 64px per the OrbForm doc's own "Pyre's 64 px" reference, `OrbForm.cs` header comment) in reasonable export time.
- **Whether "Playback3D" (the one existing Pyre `ShapeForm` that already imports external animated 3D content) is a closer analogy to Option C than treated above, or whether its stalled runtime-bake status (`Pyre.cs:609-621`) is itself evidence that "import external content as a ShapeForm" is harder to finish than it looks** — I read it as a cautionary precedent (§5, Option C) rather than a solved template; the owner may weigh this differently having lived through why Playback3D stalled.
- **Whether the owner wants Shaper's shape-layer fuse-mode richness (4-mode CSG with soft viscosity) inside Pyre at all**, versus treating Shaper purely as a one-way asset pipeline forever — this investigation surfaces the cost of each path but the owner's actual appetite for "keep two apps" vs "eventually merge" is not something either codebase can answer.
- I did not verify GAP_ANALYSIS.md's/PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md's own acceptance-criteria pass beyond spot-checking the specific claims cited above (fuse-mode schema, material schema, normal/lighting code, ShapeForm handling) — the docs are trusted per the task's own instruction to mine them first, with source confirmed only for load-bearing claims.
