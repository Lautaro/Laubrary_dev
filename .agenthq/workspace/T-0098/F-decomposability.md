## Summary

**The proposal is architecturally sound and much further along than it looks.** Of the 29 selectable techniques, **14 already compute a scalar field as a distinct step and then colour it in a separate function** — for several of them the seam is a literal function boundary you could cut today (`FireSim.Step` → `FireSim.Render`; `PyreOrb`'s energy plane → `PyreOrb.Rasterise`; `PyreInferno`'s "field pass" → "shading pass"). **13 more are routine but real work** — the flat stamps and the lit solids, where coverage and colour are interleaved per pixel but never conceptually entangled. **Exactly one is genuinely entangled** (Sprite, and by *data*, not by algorithm). One is not a generator at all (3D Playback).

**Zero generators are entangled because "colour is driven by heat/density/height".** That objection dissolves on inspection: every field generator in Pyre already carries the scalar separately from the picture, and in four places Pyre *already* pushes an energy scalar into the fill in place of the life clock (`PyreRenderer.cs:1659`, `:1870`, `FireSim.cs:295`). The proposal's shape stage exporting `{coverage, energy, height, normal, distance-to-edge}` is a description of what the code already computes; it just never names it or lets anything else read it.

**The genuine difficulties are elsewhere, and they are not small.** (1) For seven of the nine plug-in scenes the *silhouette is decided by the material stage*, not handed to it — swap the fill and the shape changes. (2) `ZuiFill` is a sealed `[Serializable]` class with a fixed four-mode enum, not a `[SerializeReference]` polymorphic type; "pluggable fills" is a new type system, not a rename. (3) Today's fill is evaluated **per particle, in the particle's own frame, on the particle's own clock** — one fill across a fused group is a different feature that already exists in exactly one place (Coalesce) and already documents what it costs.

**On grouping: Pyre already has it and it has never been used.** WriteMatte → numbered channel with Max/Add/Subtract → a Draw layer with `heightFromChannel` is precisely "several shape layers combined into one silhouette that ONE fill is then applied to" (`PyreRenderer.cs:908-919`, `:1849-1883`). Measured usage across all 305 saved layers: **zero** (`D-verification.md:2.1`). The owner's grouping idea is not new construction — it is a re-frame of dead machinery, plus two additions it genuinely lacks (a soft-blend combine, and a group transform).

**One live bug found on the way** (§Part 4, case 9): editing a Fire, Fireball or Text layer's Fill gradient in the editor almost certainly has **no effect on the render**, because those three read the frozen legacy `ZuiFill.gradient` while the editor writes `ZuiFill.gradientAnim.gradient`, and the two stop being the same object the first time the ramp control fires.

### Prose on the entangled and near-entangled cases

**Sprite is the only true ENTANGLED.** Its shape and its colour are the same authored artefact. There is no scalar the shape stage could export that a fill could meaningfully read — the interior is a bitmap, not a field. Every other generator in Pyre answers "what would the fill read?" with a number; Sprite answers "the artwork". Under the new model Sprite stops being a *fill provider* and becomes the best available **shape provider**: a silhouette imported from outside. That is a promotion, not a loss, and it is the same slot `PYRE_GUG.md` §8 wants Shaper to occupy.

**The lit solids are NOT entangled, and this is the finding that most changes the shape of the proposal.** `A-generator-inventory.md` and `PYRE_GUG.md` §9 both treat them as the expensive case ("their lighting is computed and consumed in the same place"). That is true line-by-line and misleading architecturally, because `DrawFacetSolid` and `DrawOrb` **already have five separate `ZuiFill`s** — `shapeFill` (called `matFill`, *"material fill"*, in the code itself, `PyreRenderer.cs:4279`), `gemSpecularFill`, `gemInnerGlowFill`, `gemLineFill`, `gemEdgeGlowFill` (`:4275-4278`) — plus a full authored light rig (`gemLightYaw/Pitch/Distance`, `gemAmbient/Diffuse/Specular/SpecPower`, `:4264-4291`). A material stage for the solids is not something to invent; it is something to **move**. What must move with it is the lighting model, because `lit` is currently computed by the shape (`:4341`, `:4538`) and multiplied into the material colour. That is the one real design decision: **does the light live in the shape stage or the material stage?** If it lives in the material, "a Box wearing churning plasma" works and the plasma is lit; if it lives in the shape, the material receives a pre-lit scalar and a churning plasma will look flat-lit. Recommendation: the light belongs to the **material**, and the shape exports the normal — that is what `DrawOrb` already computes analytically (`:4534`) and what `PyreInferno` fakes from its own density gradient (`PyreInferno.cs:758-763`).

**"Needs an extra channel" vs "genuinely inseparable" — the answer.** I found **no** generator in the second category on energy grounds. Every case that looks like it (Fire's heat, Ramp's density+heat, Jet's H+T, Inferno's density+heat+soot, Arc Burst's E+A) is a generator that already keeps the scalar in its own array and colours it in a second pass. What the shape stage must export is small and finite: **coverage, one or two energy scalars, height/depth, a normal, distance-to-edge, and a small handful of per-form indices** (glyph index and line coordinate for Text; slab index for any extrusion; per-particle depth `zNorm` for anything swarmed). Six of those seven are already computed somewhere in `PyreRenderer.cs` or `PyreField.cs` today.

## PART 2 — What already exists

### 2.1 FILL — a real abstraction, one type, not polymorphic

**What it is.** A Pyre layer's colour is `public ZuiFill shapeFill` (`RT/Pyre/Pyre.cs:324`), defined at `ZUI/Scripts/Runtime/ZuiFill.cs:24`. It is a `[Serializable] class` implementing `ISerializationCallbackReceiver` — **not** `[SerializeReference]`, so it is one concrete type with a fixed menu, not a plug-in point.

**What it can be** (`ZuiFill.cs:29-37`):
- `Mode { Solid, OverLife, Linear, Radial }` — a flat colour, a ramp over a 0..1 clock, or a spatial ramp (linear axis with angle, or radial).
- `TextureKind { None, Sprite, Noise, Grid, Dots }` — a *replacement* for the mode, deliberately non-recursive (a texture never nests a fill).
- `FillSpace { Stamped, Fixed }` (`:48`) — **already the "per-shape vs per-canvas" axis the grouping question needs**: Stamped feeds shape-local `(u,v)` so the pattern travels with the shape; Fixed feeds canvas-anchored `(u,v)` so the shape moves *through* a stationary pattern (`PyreRenderer.cs:4987-4992`).
- `FillFit { Uniform, Stretch }` (`:65`) — how a non-square box normalises onto −1..1.
- Animatable companions: `gradientAnim` (a `[SerializeReference] ZuiGradient` with non-destructive reverse/hue/sat/brightness/contrast/quantise/cycle transforms, each a `ZUIValue` over life — `ZUI/Scripts/Runtime/ZuiGradient.cs:66-79`), `zoomAnim`, `centerXAnim`, `centerYAnim` (`ZuiFill.cs:105-107`).

**Its contract is `Color Evaluate(float life, float u, float v)`** (`ZuiFill.cs:167`). Note what that signature does *not* take: coverage, energy, height, normal, or distance-to-edge. **This is the single most important fact for the proposal.** The fill's input today is a clock and a position — nothing about the substance.

**And Pyre already cheats around that, in four places, by overloading `life`.** `RenderPlusFusedField` calls `fill.Evaluate(frac, 0f, 0f)` where `frac` is the field value (`PyreRenderer.cs:1659`). `RenderHeightConsumer` calls `fill.Evaluate(h, 0f, 0f)` where `h` is the fused height (`:1870`). `RenderPlusRampField` shades by `clamp01(density+heat)·light` (`:1687`). `FireSim.Render` calls `ramp.Evaluate(t)` where `t` is heat (`RT/SpriteFx/FireSim.cs:301`). **So the same `shapeFill` field means "colour over the particle's life" on a Disc and "colour over heat" on a Fire, and nothing in the UI distinguishes them.** The consequence is real: a fill whose `ZuiGradient` has an animated hue-shift over life will, in Fuse mode, have its hue driven by field density instead of by time — because `EvalGrad(t, life)` (`ZuiFill.cs:235`) receives `life = frac`.

**Per-generator colour.** The lit solids carry four *additional* fills of their own (`Pyre.cs`; used at `PyreRenderer.cs:4275-4278`). Text carries `textFill` + `textBorder` + a `TextFillMode` scope enum (`Pyre.cs:406-421`). Sprite carries `spriteTint` (`Pyre.cs:456-457`). Seven of the nine plug-ins ignore the layer fill entirely (`UsesFill => false`: `ArcBurstForm.cs:50`, `OrbForm.cs:51`, `PlasmaBloomForm.cs:121`, `TorchForm.cs:55`, `Jet/JetFormBase.cs:23` covering three) and carry their own LUTs/ramps.

**How pluggable: not at all, today.** `ZuiFill` is sealed by its own comment as non-recursive; there is no `PyreFill` base class, no `[SerializeReference]` fill field anywhere in `RT/Pyre/`. "A churning-plasma fill" is a **new polymorphic type hierarchy**, not a new enum case on `ZuiFill` — and the seven `UsesFill => false` forms are the proof, because each of them needed a *richer* material than `ZuiFill` can express and solved it by opting out.

**Renaming vs new build, for FILL:** the *authoring surface* (ramps, spatial modes, textures, animated transforms, the `ZuiFillControl` UI at `ZUI/Toolkit/ZuiFillControl.cs`) is done and good. What is new: (a) a polymorphic fill/material base with `[SerializeReference]`; (b) a substance-aware `Evaluate` signature; (c) multi-ramp materials (Jet needs two ramps + two ceilings crossfaded by soot).

### 2.2 BORDER — a real, generator-agnostic pass, artificially gated to 6 of 29

**Pyre already has a first-class border, and it is built exactly the way the proposal wants.** Fields on the layer (`RT/Pyre/Pyre.cs:504-528`): `borderEnabled`, `borderWidth` (a `ZUIValue`, animatable over life), `borderFill` (a full `ZuiFill`), `borderOverMatte`.

The implementation is a **silhouette-outline post-pass over the layer's finished alpha** (`BuildBorderBuffer`, `PyreRenderer.cs:995-1045`), fed by a chamfer inside-distance transform (`BorderInsideDistance`, `:958-985`) that gives every pixel its approximate Euclidean distance to the nearest transparent pixel. The rim alpha is `borderFill.a × band weight × the shape's own coverage`, so it inherits the shape's antialiasing (`:1035`). It returns a **separate buffer**, composited either into the layer or deferred over the finished frame (`PyreRenderer.Layers.cs:170-177`).

The design comment states the seam in the proposal's own language: *"it (a) works identically on all six forms with no per-form edits, and (b) keeps the **FILL and the BORDER as separate buffers**"* (`PyreRenderer.cs:946-948`).

**The gate.** `p.hasBorder = layer.form == null && layer.borderEnabled && IsFlat2DBorderForm(layer.shapeForm)` (`PyreRenderer.Layers.cs:92`), where `IsFlat2DBorderForm` is a hardcoded six-case predicate — Disc, Crescent, Ring, Streak, Star, Polygon (`PyreRenderer.cs:950-952`). This is the predicate `B-compatibility.md:118` flags as the existing template for a capability system. **Nothing in `BuildBorderBuffer` reads the generator.** It reads a `Color32[]`. The gate is policy, not capability — it exists because the solids have their own edge lines, Text has its own SDF border, and nobody wanted a rim on Fire.

**Other edge treatments that exist:**
- **Lit-solid edge lines + two glows** — `isLine` from `edist ≤ gemLineWidth` coloured by `gemLineFill`; a halo from `edgeGlow`/`gemEdgeGlowFill` drawn *inside and outside*; an inner glow from `gemInnerGlowFill` (`PyreRenderer.cs:4345-4380`, `:4552-4571`). Per-form, hardcoded, not a plugin.
- **Text's SDF border band** — `borderBand` in SDF value units, switching `layer.textFill` → `layer.textBorder` (`:3994-3999`, `:4025-4027`). Per-form, hardcoded.
- **`edgeSoftness`** (`Pyre.cs`, evaluated `PyreRenderer.cs:2912-2914`) — a rim feather, honoured by the six flat forms, ignored by the solids, Sparkle, Sprite, Text, Fire, Fireball, Playback3D (`ED/Pyre/PyreWindow.cs:1320-1322`).
- **SpriteFx** — `OutlineModifier` (`RT/SpriteFx/SpriteFxModifiers.cs:1887`) and `EdgeSmoothModifier` (`:2734`) as post passes; `SpriteFxAuxMap` already derives Sobel edges and a **chamfer distance field** from a finished buffer (`RT/SpriteFx/SpriteFxAuxMap.cs:16-27`, `:39-55`). Note: `D-verification.md:§3` confirms Pyre never pads its canvas for `OutwardReachPx`, so Outline in Pyre is clipped at the canvas edge.
- **`EdgeModifier`/`EdgeWarpModifier`** (`SpriteFxModifiers.cs:2565`, `:2585`) — a declared silhouette-edge stage with **no caller anywhere** (`B-compatibility.md:§2`, re-verified in `D:§3`). Dead, but it is the one place someone previously reached for exactly this concept.

**Is there a pluggable border concept today? No — but there are two working implementations of the mechanism** (Pyre's `BuildBorderBuffer` + SpriteFx's `AuxMap` chamfer), and a dead stage declaration that wanted to be one. **Renaming vs new build, for BORDER:** the pass, the distance transform, the separate-buffer discipline and the `borderOverMatte` deferral all exist. What is new: making it polymorphic (`bevel`, `rim light`, `burning border` as types rather than one recolour), removing the six-form gate, and giving it a signed distance rather than an inside-only distance so it can bloom outward.

### 2.3 EXTRUSION / HEIGHT — three unrelated implementations, no shared concept

1. **Height relief from a swarm** — `Coalesce = Ramp` builds `height = density·2.65 + heat·0.72` and lights it via `PyreField.ReliefLight` (`PyreRenderer.cs:1697-1837`; helper `RT/Pyre/PyreField.cs:191`).
2. **Height from a matte channel** — `heightFromChannel >= 0` makes a Draw layer discard its own shape and render the accumulated channel as a relief-lit heightmap through its Fill, using **the same** `PyreField.ReliefLight` (`PyreRenderer.cs:1849-1883`).
3. **Real 3-D geometry** — `DrawFacetSolid`'s vertex/face models with backface culling (`:4152`, `:4182-4184`, `:4207-4211`), `DrawOrb`'s analytic sphere depth `z = √(R²−d²)` (`:4532`), `DrawRing`'s tilted plane.
4. **True 2-D-silhouette extrusion — exactly one instance, in Text.** `DrawTextChar` marches 8 depth slabs (`DepthSteps = 8`, `:3946`), solves the inverse screen map per slab (`:4011-4017`), takes the first (front-most) hit, and darkens side faces by `0.45 × lerp(1, 0.55, k/8)` (`:4029-4032`). Depth is `textDepth × char size` (`Pyre.cs:426`).

**What "extrusion plugins applicable to any shape" would require that does not exist.** Item 4 is structurally generic — it needs only (a) a 2-D coverage or SDF field it can sample at an arbitrary `(u,v)`, (b) a depth, (c) a rotation basis — but it is welded to the TMP glyph sampler `SampleTextSdf` (`:4020`) and to per-char geometry. Generalising it needs: a **resamplable** shape stage (today most rasters are one-shot loops that write pixels, not functions you can query at `(u,v)`), a depth-slab march or a proper height-field sweep, and a side-face shading rule that is a *material* concern (today it is a hardcoded 0.45 constant). Items 1-2 are height-*map* relief, not extrusion — they fake depth from a scalar's slope and cannot produce a silhouette that grows with depth. Item 3 is real 3-D but each solid is one hardcoded primitive; `C-shaper3d.md:§4` is right that Pyre has **no** way to extrude an arbitrary silhouette. **Verdict: extrusion is essentially a new build, with one worked example (Text) to copy and one existing depth-lighting helper (`ReliefLight`) to reuse.**

### 2.4 GROUPING — the critical question, answered

**Does ANY existing mechanism combine SILHOUETTES before colour? Yes — two, and both are unused or nearly so.**

**(a) Coalesce = Fuse / Ramp — within one layer's swarm.** `RenderSwarm` collects each alive particle as `FieldParticle{pos, radius, weight}` or `RampParticle{pos, radius, density, heat, alpha}` **instead of drawing it** (`PyreRenderer.cs:1553-1584`), then a single field pass reads the whole set and shades one merged silhouette through one fill. This is genuinely pre-colour: no particle is ever coloured. **Its limits, in code:** the chosen form is thrown away — *"The form is ignored in both modes: a coalescing particle is always a circle/dome"* (`:1552-1553`); it works only within one layer; and it is unreachable for plug-in forms, Fire, Fireball, Playback3D and swarm-off layers because `RenderLayer` returns before `RenderSwarm` in every one of those cases (`:248-283`). Used on 20 of 305 saved layers (`D-verification.md:§3`).

**(b) The matte channel system — across layers.** `WriteMatteCoverage` folds a layer's finished **alpha** (or luminance × alpha when `matteWriteLuma`) into one of four `float[]` planes with `MatteCombine { Max, Add, Subtract }` (`PyreRenderer.cs:908-919`; enum at `Pyre.cs:164`). A later Draw layer either multiplies its own alpha by that plane (`clipByChannel`, `CompositeLayer :922-937`) or — with `heightFromChannel` — **discards its own shape entirely and renders the combined plane through one Fill** (`RenderHeightConsumer :1849-1883`).

**That second path is, precisely, the owner's grouping model.** Several layers contribute silhouettes; Max/Add/Subtract combines them (union / accumulate / carve — three of Shaper's four fuse modes, missing only soft-blend); one Fill colours the result; relief lighting gives it body. It works today. **Measured usage: `matteEnabled: 1` on 0 of 305 layers; `heightFromChannel >= 0` on 0 of 305** (`D-verification.md:§2.1`).

**The catch, and it is the real one.** Path (b) is *not* pre-colour in the way the owner means. Each source layer is fully rendered — form, border, post modifiers, sim, everything (`PyreRenderer.Layers.cs:151-178`) — and then **only its alpha (or luminance) is harvested and the RGB thrown away**. So the combination happens on finished coloured pixels; the colour work is done and discarded. That is wasteful, but more importantly it means a group member cannot contribute *energy* or *height* or a *normal* — only opacity. A genuine shape-group needs the members to hand over substance, which is the same change Part 1 requires.

**Nothing else combines before colour.** `FuseModifier` ("blob melt", `SpriteFxModifiers.cs:2459`) is a post-process blur-and-threshold on finished RGBA. `LumaMatte`/`ApplyMatte` (`PyreRenderer.cs:1099-1122`) reads finished luminance. Every layer-to-layer composite is `Over` on finished colour (`CompositeLayer :922`).

**Renaming vs new build, for GROUPING:** the combiner (`Max/Add/Subtract` into a float plane), the "one fill over a combined field" consumer, and the relief lighting all exist and are unused. What is new: soft-blend combination (Shaper's `Add soft` / `Subtract soft` viscosity — `C-shaper3d.md:§2` correctly notes Pyre's masks *cannot* do this at all), members handing over substance rather than alpha, and a group as an addressable, transformable, orderable entity.

## PART 3 — The grouping model: recommendation

### Can "a group of shape layers, textured as one" sit inside Pyre's current layer model?

**Data-model answer: yes, without a tree — and the mechanism is already written.** `Pyre.layers` is a flat `List<PyreLayer>` (`RT/Pyre/Pyre.cs:1207`) and there is no nesting anywhere. A group can be expressed as it already is: *N* consecutive layers whose role is "contribute to channel *k*", followed by one layer whose role is "consume channel *k* and colour it". That is `MatteRole.WriteMatte` + `heightFromChannel`, verbatim. Four channels exist, so four simultaneous groups.

**UX answer: no, not as it stands.** A group expressed as "a run of sibling layers plus a magic consumer, wired by an integer channel index chosen in a Matte box" is invisible, which is exactly why it has been used zero times in 305 authored layers. `PYRE_GUG.md` §3 already reaches the same conclusion about the Mass generators being "real generators wearing a disguise"; this is the same disease one level up.

### Recommendation

**Build the group as a first-class layer *kind* over the existing channel machinery — not as a nested layer tree.**

Concretely: one `PyreLayer` whose generator is **Group**, holding an ordered list of *shape members* (each: a generator + its own transform + its own combine mode + its own lifetime window), which resolve into one substance and are then coloured by that layer's single fill/border/extrusion. Members are **not** `PyreLayer`s and must not be — `C-shaper3d.md:§2` is right that conflating Shaper's colourless silhouette layer with Pyre's fully-timed render citizen is the fastest way to misdesign this, and `PYRE_GUG.md` §8 reaches the same verdict from the Shaper side. A member is a *shape*; a `PyreLayer` is a *scene participant*.

**Why one level and not a tree.** Shaper allows nesting to 4 levels / 512 pieces (`C-shaper3d.md:§2`) because a Shaper shape is a reusable library asset that can contain another. Pyre has no shape-asset concept, so nesting would buy recursion with no reuse to justify it. One level covers the stated requirement ("several shape layers combined into one silhouette … that group itself being one layer among many") exactly. If reusable silhouette assets arrive later (the Shaper import), a member can reference one and the nesting lives inside *that asset*, not inside Pyre's layer list — the same containment Shaper already uses.

**Why over the channel machinery.** The combine ops, the float-plane accumulation, the relief lighting and the "one fill over a combined field" consumer are written and debugged (`PyreRenderer.cs:908-919`, `:1849-1883`, `PyreField.cs:155-215`). Rebuilding them would be the second implementation of something with zero users; re-fronting them gives the existing code its first user.

**How the substance change and the grouping change relate.** They are the same change. A group needs members to hand over coverage-plus-energy rather than finished pixels; that is exactly what Part 1's shape stage produces. **Do not sequence them as two projects** — grouping without the substance split gives you today's alpha-only matte grouping, which is the feature nobody has used.

### The concrete problems a group must answer

1. **Ordering.** Combine order is evaluation order and it matters (Subtract is not commutative). Today `MatteCombine` is a per-*writer* field with no explicit ordering beyond layer index, and `Max` is the default — so authored order is silently significant. A group must make member order visible and reorderable, and must decide whether the combine op lives on the member (Shaper's model — `C-shaper3d.md:§2`) or on the group. **Recommend: on the member**, matching Shaper, so a member can be dropped in as "subtract".
2. **Per-member transforms.** Pyre has no per-layer transform at all — position comes from the swarm's spawn placement or `particlePathX/Y`, rotation from `particleSpin`, scale from `size`. A member needs an honest local transform (offset, rotation, scale) that is *not* a swarm. This is new, small, and unavoidable.
3. **Per-member animation and lifetime.** `startFrame`/`endFrame` is per `PyreLayer` (`Pyre.cs:186-195`) and every envelope evaluates at `plan.layerLife` (`PyreRenderer.Layers.cs:87`). If members get their own windows, the group's silhouette changes shape mid-clip while the fill runs on the group's clock — which is the *good* case and is what "a fireball shape wearing a churning-plasma material" means. Decide explicitly that **the fill's clock is the group's, not any member's**, or the feature collapses back to per-member colouring.
4. **Per-member swarm.** This is the sharpest one. A swarm is a per-layer sub-system with ~15 dials (`Pyre.cs:660-756`) and it multiplies one shape into hundreds. If each member may swarm, a group is a cross-product of clouds and the "one fill over one silhouette" promise means the fill is spread over a cloud of clouds. If no member may swarm, you lose the most-used feature in the tool. **Recommend: members may swarm, and a swarming member contributes its whole cloud's coverage as one contribution** — which is precisely what `Coalesce = Fuse` already does within a layer (`PyreRenderer.cs:1617`), so it is a re-use rather than a new rule. Put the group's own swarm on the *group layer* (stamp the assembled group N times), which is what Family 2's plug-in scenes already do (`A-generator-inventory.md:§6 Family B`).
5. **Masks.** A group must decide whether it can be a matte source and whether members can be clipped. Today `p.isHeightConsumer` is assigned only after the `isMatte`/`isLuma` branches have `continue`d, so **a layer is a writer or a consumer, never both** (`PyreRenderer.Layers.cs:102-105`; `D-verification.md:§2.5`). A group is a consumer by construction, so under today's code it could never also feed a mask — that restriction must be lifted deliberately or documented.
6. **Soft combine.** `MatteCombine` has no soft-blend. Shaper's `Add soft`/`Subtract soft` take a viscosity strength *and* sharpness (`C-shaper3d.md:§2`). Pyre has the primitive already: `PyreField.SmoothMax(a, b, k)` (`PyreField.cs:139`), used by the Ramp field pass. Wiring `SmoothMax` in as a fourth and fifth `MatteCombine` case is a small, high-value addition.
7. **Cost.** Each member currently costs a full `Color32[W*H]` scratch plus a full render (`PyreRenderer.cs:231`). A substance-producing member should write a `float[]` (or two), which is cheaper, but a group of eight members is still eight full-canvas passes per frame per group. Worth measuring before promising it.

## PART 4 — The hard cases against the proposal

Ordered roughly by how much they hurt. Each: what it is, why it hurts, and any answer I can see.

**1. For seven of the nine plug-in scenes, the material *decides the silhouette* — coverage is an output of the fill, not an input to it.**
`PyreOrb.Rasterise` computes `tone = 1 − exp(−E·gain)` then `a = ((tone − a0)/(a1 − a0))^acurve · amax`, then a floor cut and a despeckle (`RT/Pyre/Forms/Kiln/PyreOrb.cs:107-118`, `:613`). `PyreJetEngine`: `alpha = smoothstep((H − lo)/soft) · ceiling` (`Jet/PyreJetEngine.cs:32-33`). `PyreTorch`: *"alpha = smoothstep on the raw heat"* (`PyreTorch.cs:7`). `PyreArcBurst`: the alpha plane is *"zeroed under the lowest energy band"* (`PyreArcBurst.cs:9`). `PyreInferno` and `PyrePlasmaBloom` are the same shape.
**Why it hurts:** the model's core promise is "swap the fill, keep the shape". For these seven, swapping the fill *changes the shape* — often drastically, because the alpha window is what turns a smooth exponential energy field into a readable bolt or a jet tongue. It also means the shape stage cannot answer "what is this layer's silhouette?" without running a material. That breaks the group model (a member's contribution is undefined until it is coloured), breaks the border pass (which needs a silhouette), and breaks the compatibility badges `B-compatibility.md:§2` proposes.
**Possible answer:** split the material into two declared halves — an **opacity transfer function** (energy → alpha: window, curve, floor, despeckle) that belongs to the *shape* stage and travels with it, and a **colour transfer function** (energy → RGB: the LUT/ramps) that is the swappable fill. That is a genuine reading of the existing code — `OrbStyle` already separates `a0/a1/acurve/amax` from `lut`, and `JetShade` already separates the alpha smoothstep from the LUT lookup. It is not free: it means the pluggable "fill" is narrower than "the material", and a plasma fill cannot make a solid shape wispy.

**2. A single `ZuiFill` cannot express the materials seven generators already need — so "fill" must become a polymorphic type, and `ZuiFill` is not one.**
`ZuiFill` is a `[Serializable] class` with a closed `Mode` enum and a closed `TextureKind` enum, explicitly designed to be non-recursive (`ZUI/Scripts/Runtime/ZuiFill.cs:24-37`). The Jet family's shade pass needs **two whole ramps and two opacity ceilings crossfaded by a soot ratio** (`Jet/PyreJetEngine.cs:29-33`). Inferno needs a 256-entry LUT baked at `1 − heat` plus a smoke-grey ↔ fire-ramp mix plus a pseudo-normal light (`PyreInferno.cs:537-544`, `:749`). Arc Burst needs five cel bands by threshold. Plasma Bloom needs two LUTs selected by radius. All seven solved this by declaring `UsesFill => false` (`ArcBurstForm.cs:50`, `OrbForm.cs:51`, `PlasmaBloomForm.cs:121`, `TorchForm.cs:55`, `Jet/JetFormBase.cs:23`).
**Why it hurts:** the seven `UsesFill => false` declarations are not laziness; they are seven independent verdicts that the layer fill is not expressive enough. Promising "any fill on any shape" means either making `ZuiFill` far richer (and it is used by ~everything in Laubrary, so every change is a compatibility risk) or introducing a `PyreMaterial` base with `[SerializeReference]` and accepting that `ZuiFill` becomes *one* material among several. The latter is right but is a new type system with migration, cloning, content-hashing (`PyreForm.cs:325` already hashes fills for cache invalidation) and editor support.
**Possible answer:** `PyreMaterial` base, `[SerializeReference]`, with `ZuiFillMaterial` (wraps today's behaviour, default, byte-identical), `DualRampMaterial` (the Jet/Inferno shape), `CelBandMaterial` (Arc Burst), `LitSolidMaterial` (the gem block). The `PyreForm` plug-in contract is the exact precedent — nine forms, `[SerializeReference]`, `[MovedFrom]` for migration, `Clone()`, `ContentHash()` (`RT/Pyre/PyreForm.cs:182-290`).

**3. The swarm: a fill is applied per particle today, in the particle's own frame, on the particle's own clock — and the one place Pyre already fuses a swarm documents exactly what that costs.**
In `DrawParticle` the fill is evaluated at the **particle's own life** and, when spatial, at the **particle-local** `(dx/radius, dy/radius)` (`PyreRenderer.cs:2900-2902`, and the same pattern in every `Draw*Body`). `ZuiFill.FillSpace.Stamped` (the default) exists specifically to name this; `Fixed` is the canvas-anchored alternative and it already works (`PyreRenderer.cs:4987-4992`). So a 200-particle swarm today is 200 independent fill evaluations at 200 lives in 200 frames.
**Why it hurts:** "one fill over the group" is a *different picture*, not a refinement. `RenderPlusFusedField` — the only code in Pyre that does it — states both losses in its own comments: *"Depth brightness (brightMul) is intentionally not carried: fuse merges silhouettes, colour comes from the field, not per-particle depth"* and *"a spatial fill degrades to its centre-line sample"* (`PyreRenderer.cs:1655-1658`). Per-particle lifetime-driven colour fades (the default `OverLife` fire ramp on a Disc swarm — 78% of all authored layers are Discs, `D-verification.md:§3`) simply cease to exist under a single group fill, because there is no longer a per-particle life to sample.
**Possible answer:** make it an explicit, named choice on the group/layer — *per-member fill* (today) vs *one fill over the assembly* (Coalesce's behaviour) — and, in the second mode, have the shape stage export a **per-pixel "age" channel** (the life of whichever particle dominates that pixel) so a lifetime fade is still expressible. That channel does not exist today and is not free, but it is the difference between "grouping loses fades" and "grouping keeps them".

**4. Additive/glow: several generators emit colour where coverage is zero, and "colour inside a silhouette" has no seat for it.**
The lit solids' halo: `DrawFacetSolid`'s `else` branch fires when `hasFace == false` and writes `edgeGlowColor × brightMul` with alpha `haloAmt × alphaEnv` (`PyreRenderer.cs:4396-4408`); `DrawOrb` does the same (`:4585`ff). Inferno's ignition flash and embers composite *after* the shading pass, off the heat LUT, at positions the density field may not cover (`PyreInferno.cs:877`, `:882-910`). Every energy-field plug-in blooms its field with a box blur *before* thresholding (`PyreArcBurst.cs:7-8`, `PyreOrb.cs:6-7`), so the visible extent exceeds any "silhouette" you could name.
**Why it hurts:** if the shape stage's coverage is the mask and the fill paints inside it, glows are simply deleted. Glows are a large part of why these forms look the way they do. It also interacts with case 1: the bloom is applied to the *substance*, so "silhouette" is only defined post-bloom, i.e. post-material.
**Possible answer:** coverage must be an unbounded emission field, not a 0..1 stencil — i.e. the substance carries `coverage` *and* `emission`, and the material may paint where `emission > 0` and `coverage == 0`. Equivalently: accept that a "glow" is a **border/edge plugin that reaches outward**, which is what SpriteFx's `OutwardReachPx` already models (`SpriteFxModifiers.cs:1790`, `:1891`). Note Pyre does not honour `OutwardReach` at all today (`D-verification.md:§3`, upgraded to CONFIRMED), so this needs the canvas-padding fix regardless.

**5. Timing: there is no clock today on which a fill could animate independently of its shape.**
The fill's `life` argument is whatever the caller passes. Stamps pass the particle's `own` life. Field passes pass the **energy scalar** in the life slot (`PyreRenderer.cs:1659`, `:1870`). Fire passes nothing — it reaches past `ZuiFill.Evaluate` entirely and calls `ramp.Evaluate(t)` on the raw `Gradient` (`FireSim.cs:301`). Layer envelopes evaluate at `plan.layerLife` (`PyreRenderer.Layers.cs:87`); the spec has one `frameCount` (`Pyre.cs:1186`).
**Why it hurts:** "a fireball shape wearing a churning plasma material, the plasma animating on its own clock" requires a second time source per layer, plus a decision about what happens when the shape's lifetime window is 8 frames and the plasma's loop is 24. No generator today has two clocks. There is no per-layer time-remap, no sub-clock, no phase offset field.
**Partial answer, and this one is genuinely good news:** `ZuiFill` **already separates the ramp-position axis from the animation clock**. `EvalGrad(t, life)` calls `gradientAnim.Evaluate(t, 0f, life)` (`ZuiFill.cs:235`), and `ZuiGradient` animates hue/saturation/brightness/contrast/phase over `life` as independent `ZUIValue`s, with a `cycle`/`cycleSpeed` pair for palette cycling (`ZuiGradient.cs:66-79`, `:85`). So the *machinery* for "the ramp position comes from the substance, the ramp's own transforms run on a clock" is written. What is missing is (a) a caller that passes a different `life` than the shape's, and (b) the `phase` argument — every Pyre call site passes `0f`, so the palette-cycle driver has never been engaged in Pyre. Adding a material clock is a field plus a call-site change, not an engine change.

**6. Order of operations: two generators antialias *after* colouring, and the substance model wants to antialias before.**
`PyreTorch`: the heat field is evaluated on a 3× supersampled grid, coloured through a **hard 7-band palette**, and only then *"box-filtered in PREMULTIPLIED space"* (`PyreTorch.cs:6-8`). `PyrePlasmaBloom`: *"downsample (2x, premultiplied box) … one quantisation, after the mean"* (`PyrePlasmaBloom.cs:25`). `PyreOrb`'s despeckle runs on finished alpha (`PyreOrb.cs:636-644`).
**Why it hurts:** these are bit-exact ports of Python originals whose *entire visual signature* includes where the filter sits. Downsample the substance and then colour it, and a 7-band posterisation yields blurred band boundaries instead of the source's crisp stepped edges — a different picture. If the substance is the handover point, the material must be told whether it runs before or after the resample, which means the "clean" seam has an ordering parameter.
**Possible answer:** let a shape declare that its substance is supersampled and hand over at the supersampled resolution, with the resample happening after the material. Costs 9× the memory for Torch. Or: accept per-form exceptions, which is what the codebase does today.

**7. The plug-in contract hands over finished pixels, and there are ~10,600 lines behind it.**
`public abstract void Render(in PyreFormCtx ctx, Color32[] target)` (`RT/Pyre/PyreForm.cs:224`). `PyreFormCtx` carries `W, H, life, seed, layerSalt, fill, alpha, swarm[], geo[], pix[], phase, frameIndex, frameCount` (`:96-109`) and no output but the buffer.
**Why it hurts:** every one of the nine plug-ins is a bit-exact port of a Kiln Python program with hash-gated fidelity. Changing the contract to `RenderSubstance(ctx, float[] coverage, float[] energy, …)` touches all nine and every one of their `Prepare`/`Clone`/`ContentHash` paths, and each has its own pixel-modifier call convention inside its shade pass (`ctx.pix`, e.g. `PyreInferno.cs:815`). It also interacts with `HandlesGeometry` (`InfernoForm.cs:19` is the only opt-out) and with `PyrePrepassCache` sharing between a form and its render clones (`PyreForm.cs:208-214`).
**Possible answer:** additive, not breaking — add `virtual bool ProducesSubstance => false` and `virtual void RenderSubstance(…)`, default-implemented by calling `Render` and deriving coverage from the resulting alpha. Forms opt in one at a time; the seven whose shade pass is already a separate function (Orb, Jet ×3, Torch, Arc Burst, Plasma Bloom) opt in cheaply, and Inferno's field/shading split is already labelled.

**8. Three of Family 2 are not "a shape with a fill" in any useful sense — they are whole authored scenes.**
Explosive Jet is 1,005 lines of program with 10 named variants, +89 dials, a blast schedule, fracture, second crack, flash, chunks, gobs, dust, sparks and ring fronts (`Jet/ExplosiveJetProgram.cs`; `A-generator-inventory.md:§3.7-3.9`). Arc Burst is 187 dials over 10 layouts (`ArcBurstForm.cs`). Orb is 141 dials over 5 programs.
**Why it hurts:** "give it a different fill" is not a question their authors were answering. Their populations all deposit into shared H/T planes (verified: `ExplosiveJetProgram.cs:736`, `:905`, `:949` all set a `tint` = soot into the same shade pass), so mechanically a substance split works — but the *user-facing* promise "any shape × any fill" produces a combinatorial menu in which most cells are meaningless. A brushed-metal material on an Explosive Jet is not a feature anyone asked for.
**Possible answer:** don't promise orthogonality. Let a generator **recommend** materials and mark others as unusual — the `DisabledInPicker` greying machinery already exists (`ED/Pyre/PyreWindow.Modifiers.cs:354-360`) and `B-compatibility.md:§2` already recommends making it a function of `(thing, selected layer)`.

**9. LIVE BUG found while measuring the fill system: editing the Fill gradient of a Fire, Fireball or Text layer probably does nothing.**
`ZuiFill` keeps a legacy `public Gradient gradient` as the *frozen migration source* and a `[SerializeReference] ZuiGradient gradientAnim` as the live one; its own comment says *"The editor edits gradientAnim.gradient going forward; the legacy scalar stays the frozen source"* and `OnBeforeSerialize` deliberately never writes back (`ZuiFill.cs:69-77`, `:398-401`). `EnsureGradientAnim` seeds by **aliasing the same object**: `gradientAnim = new ZuiGradient { gradient = gradient }` (`:393-396`). But `ZuiGradientEditor` **replaces** the object on edit: `Source.RegisterValueChangedCallback(e => Mutate(() => _g.gradient = e.newValue))` (`ZUI/Toolkit/ZuiGradientEditor.cs:58`). From that moment `fill.gradient` and `fill.gradientAnim.gradient` are different objects.
And Fire, Fireball and Text read the **legacy** field directly, not `Evaluate`: `Gradient ramp = layer.shapeFill.gradient` at `PyreRenderer.cs:442` (Fire), `:616` (Fire with swarm emitters), `:798` (Fireball), and `Gradient grad = fill.gradient` at `:4084` (Text's non-texture, non-Solid path). Worse, the **content hashes** that drive the sims' replay-cache invalidation read the same stale field (`:552`, `:849`, and `PyreForm.cs:325` for plug-in caches), so a ramp edit would not even invalidate the cache.
**Confidence:** the code path is confirmed by reading; I did **not** run Unity, so I have not seen a flame fail to change colour. Anyone touching this should reproduce it before fixing. **Why it belongs here:** it is the same disease the proposal is meant to cure — a "fill" that four generators reach *past* instead of *through*, because `ZuiFill.Evaluate`'s signature never fitted what they needed.

**10. Grouping's members currently pay full colour cost and throw it away.**
A WriteMatte layer renders completely — form, border, post modifiers, sim — into isolated scratch, and then only its alpha (or luma × alpha) is harvested (`PyreRenderer.Layers.cs:151-178`, `PyreRenderer.cs:908-919`).
**Why it hurts:** a group of eight members is eight full RGBA renders per frame to produce eight scalars. At Pyre's canvas sizes this is survivable but it is pure waste, and it forecloses the interesting version (members contributing energy/height/normal rather than opacity).
**Possible answer:** a substance-producing member writes `float[]` planes and never allocates a `Color32[]`. This is a *saving* that the substance split delivers, and is worth putting in the pitch rather than leaving as an implied cost.

**11. Two counting traps for whoever writes the plan.**
`heightFromChannel` and `Coalesce = Ramp` are *not* two instances of one technique for costing purposes: `PYRE_GUG.md` §10 proposes fusing them into one "Height field", and `D-verification.md:§3` establishes that of the 20 layers using a "Mass" mode, **zero** are the mask-fed one. So that fusion merges a used technique with a never-used one, and any claim about "grouping is already used" must not lean on it. Separately, the four "projects" in the usage census are three clones of one (`D:§2.2`) — the authored corpus is ≈32 effects / ≈79 layers, so "zero uses of grouping" is zero out of 79 real layers, not out of 305.
