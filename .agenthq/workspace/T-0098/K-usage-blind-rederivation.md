# K — Usage-blind re-derivation of PYRE_GUG.md and PYRE_SHAPE_FILL_BORDER.md

Investigator K, T-0098. This is an audit of **two documents**, not of the code — but every verdict I re-ground carries a `file:line` citation checked in the working tree on `feat/lathe` as of 2026-08-30. Paths are relative to `Assets/Packages/Laubrary/` unless written in full. No source was changed, no Unity opened, no tree-mutating git run.

**The ruling being applied** (owner, verbatim): *(1) no asset that comes out of any Laubrary tool — Pyre, 3DShaper — should be taken into account; they can all be deleted if it would simplify this refactoring project. (2) The amount of assets using a particular shape, generator or spriteFX says nothing about the tech it uses. That's not the way I have used it. The fact that some of them have now assets saved using them has more to do with the speed of which I have been developing. So don't take that into account.*

Two consequences I apply mechanically throughout:

- **Usage counts are inadmissible as evidence about the value, quality, safety or discoverability of a technique.** Not "discounted" — inadmissible. This includes the tempting inverse inference "unused therefore unfindable", which the ruling explicitly forecloses ("has more to do with the speed of which I have been developing").
- **Migration cost and "an asset would break" are not objections.** Anywhere a report says "this needs a migration note", "this changes existing renders", "nothing has to be migrated to start", or "six of seven slices cannot break a saved effect", that consideration is void. It is not weakened; it is off the table.

---

## Bottom line

- **Both documents' central architectural claims survive untouched** — they never rested on usage. Report 1's root diagnosis (four unrelated mechanisms decide what a layer draws, `PyreRenderer.cs:241`; standardise on one Generator field with published contracts) and report 2's model (three kinds, two op stages, one survivor) are pure code readings.
- **What does not survive is the reports' shared theory of *what to do first and why*.** Report 2 §12 explicitly orders its route by asset safety ("Six of the seven cannot break a saved effect. Exactly one can"). That organising principle is now void. The route must be re-ordered on **uncertainty reduction and dependency**, and when you do that the ordering barely changes — but the stated reasons do, and slice 3 (name the substance) should move earlier, not stay third.
- **The anti-rewrite conclusion survives on one pillar instead of two, and the surviving pillar is scoped.** "~10,000 lines of bit-exact ports" is real and verified (`Forms/Kiln/*.cs` = 7,542 lines, plus the whole `Forms/Kiln/Jet/` subtree not in that count; `ExplosiveJetProgram.cs:14-17` documents the port as a `diff -r` against external Python). "The authored body of work this would be risked for is about 32 effects" is void. **Net: do not rewrite the nine Kiln scene generators. A clone-and-rebuild of Pyre's own layer model, renderer core and 16 built-in enum forms is now a genuinely open option that the asset-risk argument was doing most of the work to close.** That is a real change in the answer the owner is entitled to.
- **The cull list shrinks and one entry changes kind.** Fork Blast: cull **hardens to a straight delete**, on the code's own supersession statement alone (`ExplosiveJetForm.cs:16-21`), with the "migration note rather than a straight delete" caveat void. Pin Warp: cull **stands** on the technical half only (`SetFrame` has no caller — animation-dead). Vortex Field: cull **fails** — its only surviving argument is that it duplicates Swirl's authoring surface, which is a *fuse* argument, not a cull argument. 3D Playback: **cut, cleanly** — its "no runtime output" defect (`PyreRenderer.cs:269`) was always the load-bearing half.
- **Report 1 §5's mask conclusion loses its own self-declared strongest argument and is left unsupported.** The *rename* (solidity vs brightness) survives on technical grounds and remains the best clarity-per-effort item in either document. "Simplify and rename rather than extend" now has **no stated evidence at all** — the sentence that carried it was the 305-layer master-switch count.
- **Report 2 §7.2's swarm objection is stronger without its number, not weaker.** Per-particle fill evaluation is a *capability* implemented at a named seam (`ZuiFill.FillSpace.Stamped` vs `Fixed`, `PyreRenderer.cs:4980-4989`), and "one fill over the assembly" structurally deletes it. That is a capability argument, admissible in full; the 42% was decoration on it.
- **The one thing that survives whole is the discoverability argument, and it is better stated structurally.** Three features are unreachable *by construction* — the border box is never even built for 23 of 29 generators (`PyreWindow.cs:1371`), grouping is an integer channel matched across two layers inside a panel called "Matte" (`PyreWindow.cs:911, 970, 993-996, 1094-1096`), and melt-into-a-blob is a radio labelled "Coalesce" inside the Swarm panel (`PyreWindow.cs:2492-2495`). None of that needs a usage count. **Restate the argument as a property of the interface, not as an outcome.**
- **Report 1 §10's "their problem is discoverability, not value" for Ring and the built-in Orb becomes unsupported.** Both are ordinary entries in the shape picker (`PyreWindow.cs:1162` region). "Distinctive" survives; "hard to find" does not.

### 2d. The anti-rewrite conclusion — tested hard

Report 2's central claim: *"It should become the frame of Pyre. It should not be built as a replacement. A rewrite is the one route that actually fails here."* Two stated supports:

**Pillar A — bit-exact ports.** "Roughly ten thousand lines of deliberately bit-exact ports, several of whose entire visual signature depends on *where in the sequence* a step happens."
**Pillar B — "the whole authored body of work this would be risked for is about 32 effects."**

**Pillar B is void.** Pillar A must carry the conclusion alone. Re-grounding Pillar A directly:

- `Runtime/Pyre/Forms/Kiln/*.cs` alone is **7,542 lines** across 15 files, with `PyreArcBurst.cs` at 1,115 and `PyreInferno.cs` at 914. That count **excludes** the entire `Forms/Kiln/Jet/` subtree (`JetForm`, `RadialJetForm`, `ExplosiveJetForm`, `JetFormBase`, `PyreJetEngine`, `ExplosiveJetProgram`, `RadialJetProgram`, `JetDraws`, `ExplosiveJetDraws`, …), so "roughly ten thousand lines" is if anything conservative.
- The ports are fidelity-bound to an **external** source that is not in this repository: `ExplosiveJetProgram.cs:14-15` cites `D:/CODEZ/Kiln/projects/Flame/agents/agent3_fork_explosive/flame3/jet.py` and a `diff -r` result naming which files are byte-identical. `PyreInferno.cs:7-14` documents a faithful port of an HTML prototype and names what was deliberately dropped and why. `PyreClipStats.cs:3` carries a "NEVER NORMALISE PER FRAME" warning derived from a documented post-mortem.
- The knowledge encoded in these files is **not recoverable from the Pyre codebase** — it is recoverable only by re-doing the porting work against external sources, one 47-row divergence table at a time.

**Rating of the remaining grounds:**

| Ground | Asset-dependent? | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Bit-exact ports to an external, non-repo source; fidelity is the deliverable | No | **STRONG** — this alone is sufficient to forbid rewriting the nine Kiln scenes |
| Order-of-operations signatures (anti-alias after colour, at triple resolution — §7.8) | No | **STRONG**, and it lives inside the ports; it is the mechanism by which a rewrite loses them |
| Existing half-built pieces are written and debugged (border, combiner, `PixelInfo.crossFrac`, `ZuiFill`) | Partly — the "no compatibility surface" half is now void | **MEDIUM**, downgraded. Code reuse only. |
| The tool must stay usable while the work happens | No | **MEDIUM-WEAK** — real workflow cost, but a clone side-steps it entirely |
| "Every generator keeps working untouched" | Yes | **VOID** |
| "Nothing has to be migrated to start" | Yes | **VOID** |

**Verdict: the anti-rewrite conclusion survives, but only as a scoped claim, and the report overstates it as a global one.**

Stated honestly: **do not rewrite the nine Kiln scene generators — ever, under any strategy.** They are the ~7,500+ lines where fidelity to an external artefact is the point, and they are also the generators whose seam is *already* a function boundary (`F-decomposability`: 14 of 29 split cleanly, "all twelve energy-field generators including every big scene one"), so they are the *least* in need of rewriting and the *most* expensive to lose.

**But the rest of Pyre is now genuinely open.** The 16 built-in enum forms, the layer model, `RenderLayer`'s four-mechanism priority order (`PyreRenderer.cs:241`), the matte/grouping plumbing and the window are not ports of anything, have no external fidelity contract, and — with assets disposable — have no preservation requirement at all. A **clone-and-rebuild of that core, with the Kiln forms carried across as-is behind their existing `PyreForm` interface, is a defensible strategy that report 2's asset-risk argument was doing most of the work to close.** The `PyreForm` plug-in boundary is exactly the seam that makes this possible: the scenes already sit behind a stable contract, which is why they can be preserved through a core rewrite that touches everything around them.

I am not recommending the rewrite here — that is a cost call the owner makes. I am stating that **the report's dismissal of it ("a rewrite is the one route that actually fails here") is no longer supported at the scope it claims**, and a synthesis writer should present it as a live option scoped to the core, not as a closed question.

## 3. What replaces usage as evidence

Usage was serving as a proxy for two different questions the reports conflated: *"is this valuable?"* and *"is this safe to change?"* The second question is now largely moot. The first needs real substitutes.

**Rubric — apply in this order. A verdict needs at least one criterion in the top four; criteria 5-8 break ties and set priority.**

| # | Criterion | Test | Which conclusions it can carry |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Reachability** | Can a user get to this from the window at all, without being told? Is a control constructed, or gated out of existence? | Promote the Mass generators into the picker; ungate the border; cull the aux-map gate and the dead outline-perturbing effect; demote/cut 3D Playback; the entire §4 discoverability argument. **The strongest available substitute, and the one the reports under-used.** |
| **2** | **Correctness** | Is it actually broken — a dead hook, a hardcoded zero, an unreachable branch? | Cull Pin Warp; fix the four blocked-effect messages; fix the five meanings of `crossFrac`; the fire/fireball/text ramp-editing bug; the melt-into-blob control offered where `coalesce` is never read. |
| **3** | **Documented supersession** | Does the source itself say X replaces Y? | Delete Fork Blast (`ExplosiveJetForm.cs:16-21`). This is the single cleanest evidence class in the whole task and applies to exactly one item. |
| **4** | **Technical distinctiveness** | Does anything else in the library produce this result? If it were gone, could you get the picture another way? | Keep Ring, the built-in Orb, Smudge, Fire/Fireball (statefulness is unfakeable), Shaper's boolean silhouettes; keep the per-particle fill capability (§7.2). |
| **5** | **Redundancy / shared code path** | Are these two picker entries one routine, or one data model with two drivers? | All four fusions; Vortex Field into Swirl. Note: redundancy argues for **fusion**, never for deletion — deletion needs criterion 1, 2, 3 or a cost argument. |
| **6** | **Implementation maturity** | Dial count, tooltip coverage, bespoke editor UI, and — decisively — whether the author's own comment calls it a stub or a proof-of-concept. | 3D Playback (`PyreRenderer.cs:262` self-declares "PROOF-OF-CONCEPT... EDITOR-PREVIEW ONLY"); the plug-in-scene density question (OrbForm: 141 dials / 146 tooltips). |
| **7** | **Cost of keeping** | Lines, dials, picker slots, dead surface, and how much the thing forces on everything else (e.g. Playback3D forces the whole spec serial, `PyreRenderer.cs:196`). | Cull the two never-dispatched plug-in kinds and the empty folders; the "presets and a browser, not more dials" recommendation. |
| **8** | **Orthogonality / explainability** | Does keeping this make the model harder to state in one sentence? Does it force an exception into a rule? | All renames; one-picker unification; "guard the word layer"; the badge contract. |

**Two rules for applying it.**

- **Never infer discoverability from usage, in either direction.** The ruling forecloses it explicitly. Discoverability must be argued from criterion 1 — the structure of the interface — which is stronger evidence anyway because it is directly inspectable.
- **Redundancy is not a cull argument.** This is where report 1 slipped: Vortex Field's cull was "unused **and** duplicative", and once the first half is removed the second half points at fusion. Apply the same test to anything else where "nothing uses it" was doing the deleting.

## 4. The one thing that survives — restated on structural grounds

**The argument.** Three ideas that are now being proposed as a re-architecture were already built, and every one of them was expressed as a setting inside another feature's panel, with no name of its own in any picker. Report 2 proved this with usage numbers. It does not need them: **each of the three is unreachable by browsing the interface, and that is a property of the code.**

### 4.1 The border stage — not merely gated, not constructed

`PyreWindow.cs:1371` — `if (IsFlat2DBorderForm(s.shapeForm)) BuildBorderBox(s);`

For any generator not in `{Disc, Crescent, Ring, Streak, Star, Polygon}` (`PyreRenderer.cs:950-952`), the border control is **not built at all**. Not greyed out with a reason. Not shown disabled. Not present. A user authoring a lit solid, Text, Fire, Fireball, Sprite, Playback3D or any of the nine Kiln scenes has **no path from the interface to the knowledge that a border stage exists** — no row, no tooltip, no trace. The renderer adds a second, independent condition — `layer.form == null` (`PyreRenderer.Layers.cs:92`) — which excludes all nine plug-in scenes even if the form list were extended.

And on the six generators that *do* get it: when `borderEnabled` is false, the control is a bare `Z.Toggle("Border", …)` appended to `shapeBody` (`PyreWindow.cs:2682-2688`) rather than a titled box; the titled `Z.BoxKeyed("Border", …)` (`:2692`) only materialises once it is already on. So even where the feature is reachable, its resting state is one unlabelled toggle inside the Shape section.

**Structural conclusion, no usage required:** a feature that renders its own control conditionally on the very choice the user is trying to make cannot be discovered by exploration. You can only find the border by picking a Disc first.

### 4.2 Grouping — an integer that must match across two layers, inside a panel named "Matte"

The full path a user must traverse, in order:

1. Tick `matteEnabled` on layer A — only then is the panel built at all: `if (layer.matteEnabled) wrap.Add(BuildMatteBox(layer, li));` (`PyreWindow.cs:911`).
2. Inside `BuildMatteBox` (`:970`), set Role via a Segmented control to `WriteMatte` (`:983-988`). The enum is `MatteRole { Draw, WriteMatte, LumaMatte }` (`Pyre.cs:135`) — no member of which contains the word "group".
3. Set **Channel** on a `Z.MicroSlider("Channel", layer.matteChannel, 0f, 3f, …)` (`:993-996`) — an integer 0–3.
4. Optionally set `matteCombine` (`Pyre.cs:215`, `MatteCombine.Max` default) — this is the union / add / carve operation, i.e. the actual grouping semantics, presented as a combine mode on a matte.
5. On a **different, later** layer B, tick `matteEnabled` again and set `heightFromChannel` via `Z.MiniRadio` (`:1094-1096`) to the **same integer**, which makes B discard its own shape entirely (`Pyre.cs:226, 233`).

The word "group" appears nowhere. The word "combine" appears only as a matte sub-setting. The wiring is an integer the user must remember and match across two non-adjacent layers, inside a panel whose name (Matte) advertises a different feature. The only visible trace of an active grouping in the layer list is a small `→0` or `▲0` glyph in the row (`PyreWindow.cs:854`, `:860`).

**Structural conclusion:** this is not merely hard to find — it is *not findable by browsing at all*. It can only be transmitted by documentation or by being told. That is a fact about the interface, provable by reading it, with no reference to what anyone did.

### 4.3 Melt-into-a-blob — a radio labelled "Coalesce", inside the Swarm panel

`PyreWindow.cs:1182` — `static readonly string[] CoalesceChoices = { "Off", "Fuse", "Ramp" };`
`PyreWindow.cs:2492-2495` — `swarmBody.Add(Z.Field("Coalesce", coalesceTip, Z.MiniRadio(coalesceSel, CoalesceChoices, …)))`

Three structural problems, all independent of usage:

- It lives inside `swarmBody`, i.e. it is reachable only after enabling the Swarm — a *different* feature. A user who wants "melt these shapes together" has no reason to look in a panel about scattering particles.
- Its label is "Coalesce" and its options are "Fuse" and "Ramp". "Fuse" is a word the tool already uses for three unrelated things (report 1 §7, unaffected by the ruling), and "Ramp" is the tool's word for a colour gradient everywhere else. Neither option name says "merge the swarm into one silhouette" or "read the swarm as a height field", which is what they do (`Pyre.cs:270-283`).
- It is **offered where it provably cannot act**. `layer.coalesce` is read at exactly two lines in the entire runtime — `PyreRenderer.cs:1497-1498`, inside `RenderSwarm` — which `RenderLayer` returns before reaching for every plug-in form, Fire, Fireball and Playback3D (`PyreRenderer.cs:248-269`). The UI gates the control only on `swarmEnabled` (`PyreWindow.cs:2206, 2492-2495`). So on roughly half the generator list, the one visible surface this feature has **lies about what it will do**.

### 4.4 The fourth, and the reason all three happened

`RenderLayer` (`PyreRenderer.cs:241`) checks **four unrelated mechanisms** in an invisible priority order to decide what a layer draws: a plug-in form, the shape enum, `coalesce`, and `heightFromChannel >= 0` (`Pyre.cs:233`, `PyreRenderer.Layers.cs:105, :168`). Three of the four have no representation in the picker. **The three unfindable features are not three accidents — they are three instances of the same structural fact: Pyre has one picker and four dispatch mechanisms.**

### 4.5 The argument, restated for the synthesis writer

*Do not write: "these three features got zero use, so discoverability is the bottleneck."* That inference is closed by the ruling.

*Write instead:* **Three of Pyre's four "what does this layer draw?" mechanisms have no picker entry, and two of its most capable stages (border, grouping) are reachable only by first making an unrelated choice — picking a flat 2D shape, or ticking a checkbox named "Matte". This is verifiable by reading `PyreWindow.cs` alone. Any re-architecture that ships a new capability through the same kind of surface — a mode inside another panel, an integer wired across layers, a control constructed only for the generators that already support it — will be equally unreachable, regardless of how good the architecture underneath is. Structure and visibility are one deliverable.**

That is the same conclusion both reports reached, arrived at without a single usage count, and it is the stronger form of the claim because it is a statement about the code rather than a prediction about behaviour.
