## Description

**Owner's ask (5 of 6):** an LFO set to sine produces a visualised effect that looks more like a looping ramp than a sine. "What gives?"

**Related, not assumed the same:** T-0420 item 4 (a 10 Hz modulator appears to change shape, not just speed) and T-0421 (the window redraws unevenly without focus). Either could produce this, but so could a real fault in the oscillator.

**Order of work:** compare the oscillator's raw output, sampled directly, against a plain mathematical sine, independent of any display. Only then look at the display path (redraw rate, how the analyser samples and plots the modulated value).

**Done means:** the cause is named with evidence, fixed if it is a fault, and a sine is seen to look like a sine on screen.

### 2026-09-27T13:30:38Z — agent — in_progress -> in_progress
Measured, not argued. I rendered a low-pass with a sine LFO on its cutoff through the engine's own offline renderer, recorded the oscillator's output at every audio block, and compared it with a mathematical sine at the same moments.

- The oscillator's formula is exact: at every point where it is evaluated it matches a sine to 0.0001.
- At the owner's setting (0.1 Hz) the shape actually applied to the sound is a true sine: worst gap 0.000. At 1 Hz it is 0.002. So a sine LFO at 0.1 Hz is NOT producing a ramp in the engine.
- What produces the ramp is the analyser. Its time view renders only 1.5 seconds of audio and loops it. At 0.1 Hz one full cycle takes 10 seconds, so the analyser shows about a seventh of one cycle (a rising slice of the sine) over and over: a sweep up, a jump back, a sweep up. That is a looping ramp. The analyser also starts the oscillator wherever it happens to be when it measures, because 'Reset phase' is off, so which slice you get changes from one measurement to the next.
- Separately, a REAL engine fault at fast rates (this is T-0420 item 4, 'the shape changes at 10 Hz'): the oscillator is only updated once per audio block. The editor's audio block is 1024 samples, about 21 ms, and between updates the value moves in a straight line. At 10 Hz that is only 4.7 updates per cycle, so the 'sine' is really a polygon with about five corners, and because 4.7 is not a whole number the corners fall in different places every cycle. The shape measurably changes from one cycle to the next and is up to 0.22 away from a true sine (full swing is 2). The owner heard this correctly. With 256-sample blocks the same measurement gives 0.014. Fix: evaluate modulators more often than once per block. I will do that as its own change after the UI work, and verify it with the same measurement.

Next: rework the analyser's time view so it covers the sound's real play and moves at real speed (T-0423). That removes the fake ramp.

### 2026-09-27T14:07:42Z — agent — in_progress -> in_progress
CORRECTION to my entry above: the 'real engine fault at fast rates' was WRONG, and it was my measurement's fault, not the engine's.

The engine does NOT update oscillators once per audio block. It splits every block into steps of 64 samples (about 1.3 ms) and updates every modulator at each step. My first measurement only read the oscillator's value once per 1024-sample block and assumed a straight line in between. So the 'polygon with about five corners' at 10 Hz was produced by how I sampled it, not by the engine.

Re-measured at every 64-sample step: the shape actually applied matches a true sine to within 0.001 at 10 Hz and 0.004 at 20 Hz. There is no engine change to make.

What this means for the owner's reports:
- 'A sine LFO looks like a ramp' (this task): the analyser, not the engine. The old time view showed a 1.5 s slice looped. For this sound (0.77 s long), a 0.1 Hz sine really is only 8% of one cycle per play: a slow sweep. That is what is heard, and the new view now shows it truthfully over the real play. A warning next to Rate explains it when one cycle is longer than the play.
- T-0420 item 4, 'at 10 Hz the shape changes': also the old display. It sampled a third-of-a-second window every 171 ms, which cannot show a 10 Hz wave; any fast shape came out as a different jagged approximation each time.

The same undersampling mistake was about to be repeated on screen: the new per-parameter lines were being sampled once per 1024-sample block. They now sample at the engine's own 64-sample step, so they draw exactly what the engine applies.

This is a fifth entry for the list in T-0418 of times reasoning about this engine was wrong until something was measured, and this time the faulty reasoning was mine.
**Proof of done:**
