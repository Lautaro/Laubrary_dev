## Description

**Owner (2026-09-29), verbatim:** "I'm thinking about traditional audio edit capabilities like cut, paste, insert etc... But I'm not sure I want to allow destructive editing. [...] It could be solved by having a Zeq with several Klips that can be trimmed and placed in different tracks. Then the whole Zeq can be used as it sounds, or we could have a function to render to a new source file. [...] Don't build this yet. There is much queued that needs to be verified and tweaked. But I'd like to start a discussion about how to do it correctly." (Full quote at the top of the design document.)

**Research only, nothing to build.** Design document: D:/Claude@GDrive/Zounds Non-Destructive Editing Research 2026-09-29.md (first draft, 2026-09-29). It builds on the ZPOC design and on the ZPOC build in flight (T-0491, T-0493, T-0494, T-0497, T-0498); section 9 lists every point where the two touch.

**Headline findings:**
1. The owner's own model is right, and most of its data already exists: a Zequence track is one placed sound (shared from the library, or a local copy that never duplicates audio), a trim is two numbers into an untouched source, and the Klip view already draws the whole source with the untrimmed parts dimmed. No edit ever needs an intermediate file; only an explicit bake writes audio.
2. The main new work is editing UI: a Zequence-level zoom window with a ruler and overview strip, range selection, trim to selection, split, slip, audition of a selection, copy and paste, an optional ripple, two playhead kinds (a shared time cursor plus per-track source playheads) with off-screen indicators and an opt-in Follow, and curves drawn and editable inline in tracks with one focused track.
3. Real finding: curves on the waveform are read by position normalised over the TRIM, so changing a trim slides every curve onto different audio. Proposed: anchor them to seconds in the source, converted on first edit. The rest of the design depends on this.
4. Second data decision: a per-track trim override on shared tracks, so pieces of one take can share one Klip's processing.
5. Correctness catch-up (a fix, not a feature): the Zequence strip still sizes and draws tracks from rendered files, its playhead is elapsed-time based, and Render to Klip still renders each Klip first as a side effect.

14 open questions for the owner are in section 12 of the document.
