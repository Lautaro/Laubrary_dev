### 2026-09-27T13:30:38Z — agent — in_progress -> in_progress
Answer, from the code: the analyser's bands actually run from 60 Hz to 18 kHz, not 62 Hz to 13.6 kHz. It labels every fourth band, so the first label lands on the first band's centre (62 Hz) and the last on band 68 (13.6 kHz); the top three bands, up to 18 kHz, carry no label. So the range is not truncated, but the labels misrepresent it.
It is still the wrong range. Human hearing runs from about 20 Hz to 20 kHz. The 60 Hz floor was chosen when the analysis window was a quarter of its current length, and a comment still says so; at the current length the analysis can resolve down to about 6 Hz. Everything below 60 Hz (the sub-bass, where much of a low-pass or high-pass decision is heard) is currently invisible.
The labels are also no help for connecting a picture to a sound: they are band centres like 62, 83, 113 rather than round numbers anyone recognises.
Plan: cover 20 Hz to 20 kHz; label round frequencies (50, 100, 200, 500, 1k, 2k, 5k, 10k) at their true positions with faint gridlines; show the frequency and the change in dB for the bar under the mouse.
