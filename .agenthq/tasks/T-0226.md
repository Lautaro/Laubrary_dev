DotGen's engine is in and reproduces its reference exactly.

A document is a frame, a seed and a set of generators. A generator owns an area; its placement fills that area with dots -- a lattice, a packed row of variable-width boxes, or rings of points -- and usually hands each dot the cell around it. Selectors score those dots without changing anything (distance from the edge, a directional ramp or wave, a deterministic random mask). Mutators then move or remove them through a selector, in an order you set. Drawers paint the areas or the cells, filled with a ZuiFill, so a fill is a flat colour, a gradient at any angle, or a random pick from a list. Every surviving dot can carry a whole child generator, sized either to its parent's area or to the exact cell around its own dot, and never clipped to either.

That last part is what makes the thing generic rather than a skyline tool. I built the acceptance scenario as a probe and it came out as three rows of buildings with lit windows -- boxes growing up through the row behind them, a grid of windows landing on each box -- without the engine containing the words building, window or row.

The demonstration composition evaluates to 308 visible dots across 32 generator areas, which is the number the behaviour work predicted before any of this existed, arrived at independently. Two renders of one seed are byte-identical; changing the seed moves 42,623 pixels. Evaluating and rendering the whole thing at 512 pixels takes 3.5 milliseconds, so the preview can redraw on every repaint without a cache. A document saves to disk and comes back with every module intact.

There is no window yet -- this is the engine only. Nothing appears in the Laubrary menu.

DECISIONS I MADE (owner away; recorded rather than parked)

1. Module ids keep the POC's prefixes (gen_, place_, sel_, mut_, draw_, fill_ + base36) instead of the g_/s_/m_/d_ shape in DOTGEN-DESIGN-DECISIONS.md section 2. A child's spawn draw is hash01(seed+37, dotIndex, childId.Length), so id LENGTH is an input to the picture: with 3-character ids the demo evaluates to different counts than the checklist measured, i.e. the tool would fail to reproduce its own reference. ApplyDemo() writes its ids literally (gen_1 .. mut_e, nextId 15) so the demo is reproducible whatever the counter did first.

2. The Margin selector has no 'zone' field even though the HTML data carries one. It is algebraically inert -- lerp(core,band,w) and lerp(band,core,1-w) are the same expression -- so it can never change a weight. The demo's Safe frame is authored exactly as spec section 20.1 states and yields the reference's 56 root dots.

3. Nudge's Bias angle is always visible; the POC hides it while bias is zero. [ZUIShowIf] matches a sibling's VALUE and 'greater than zero' is not a value. Hand-gating it inside one card would hide the rule from every other module. Left visible with a tooltip saying it does nothing at bias 0. If W1.2 adds a comparison form to ZUIShowIf this is its first customer.

4. selectorId on DotMutator and DotDrawer is [HideInInspector] so ZuiReflect skips it (ZuiReflect.cs:133) -- a reference is a picker over declared names, never the text field reflection would give it. The card draws that row.

5. The renderer has no gizmo pass and no gizmo flag at all, so an export cannot contain one by construction.
