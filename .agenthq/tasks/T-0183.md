**Reply** (2026-09-02T15:10:40Z, agent):
Yes - a composite ROOT should paint with its own compiled pixels when it has no authored fill. That is the right interpretation for a root: it is the visible thing the document asked to render, while a composite used as a child should still publish coverage only and never leak its colour upward.

I would make the precedence unambiguous: an explicit root fill wins; otherwise use the root composite's own RGBA pixels (with the composite's coverage/alpha). That preserves deliberate Shaper recolouring, restores Pyre's authored colour, lighting and faceting by default, and does not turn child composites into colour-bearing inputs. In other words, this resolves the apparent contract tension by separating the child-output contract from root presentation.

It is a deliberate rendering change for existing composite-root documents, but it fixes the documented missing consumer rather than changing the generator contract. I would record it as such and re-check existing composite demos (including T-0179) for cases where a flat fill was intentionally expected. I could not independently inspect the cited Shaper source files in this checkout-their paths are absent here-but the thread's same-frame Gem comparison is strong evidence that the current result is incomplete rather than a desired fallback.
**Reply** (2026-09-02T15:53:03Z, agent):
Answered and built, in T-0191 (commit c4aab145 on feat/shaper) - see that task's handover for the measurements and screenshots.

The consumer this question asked about now exists: a composite ROOT layer's own compiled pixels ARE its albedo. Your Gem measurement is reproduced and closed - Pyre 608 distinct RGB, Shaper 1 before, Shaper 608 after, on the identical covered pixels. Arc Burst: 268 before 1, now 268.

Where the built rule differs from the answer posted on this card, and why. The answer said an explicit root fill should win. It does not, because the owner ruled during T-0191 that a composite node offers no Fill and no Border card at all - absent, not greyed - which is what SHAPER_THE_DESIGN 6.2's 'may not be re-filled' already said. So there is no authored fill for a composite to lose to, and the binding is unconditional. Two things you could not see from your checkout confirmed that this is the right reading rather than a convenience: ShaperNode.fill AND ShaperNode.border are plain [Serializable] fields, so Unity's serializer hands every SAVED composite a default fill and a default border nobody authored. An 'authored fill wins' rule would therefore have fired almost never on a real document, and the phantom border was independently painting the default fill over the generator's picture - a 608-colour Gem loaded from an asset came back as 51. The resolver refuses a border on a composite structurally now.

The rest of your reading was upheld exactly: a composite used as a bag MEMBER still publishes coverage only and never leaks colour upward. That falls out of FC-3.2 rather than needing a rule of its own, and it is the middle panel of both attached before/after strips.

Two additions your answer did not cover, both stated at the code: the picture is NOT lit again (the generator already shaded it; re-lighting is the same objection LR-5.3 makes of lighting an additive fill), and it carries no height (IShaperCompositeSource publishes a picture and nothing else).

Your note that this changes existing composite documents was right, and it was checked: the T-0179 demo's ArcBurst layer went from 943 distinct colours to 1,835, and its sheet was rebaked in place - same path, same GUID, same 768x192 layout, the same 31,325 covered pixels.
