### 2026-09-02T22:31:31Z — agent — in_progress -> done
A generator hosted in Shaper now paints what the same generator paints in Pyre. Measured against Pyre's own window path at 128x128 over 16 frames, 25 of 27 sources match: all 16 legacy Pyre shapes are byte-for-byte identical, and all nine composite generators match Pyre in coverage, alpha and colour count.

What was wrong was one thing in three places. Pyre builds a generator's render context from the LAYER it sits on - the layer's Shape Fill, the layer's Alpha envelope at that moment, and the clip's real frame index and count. The hosting handed it none of those: no fill, opacity pinned at 1, and frame 0 of 1. Each stub was a separate thing you saw.

Fork Blast and Inferno were white and grey because they are the only two of the nine that take their colour from the layer's fill; the other seven carry their own ramps, which is why only those two looked unpainted. Orb did nothing because it reads the frame index directly - pinned at frame 0, it drew the same picture at every point in the animation. Plasma Bloom's dials looked dead for the same reason: it sizes its internal clock from the frame count, and at a count of 1 that collapsed to its lower limit, so nothing you turned could move it. Seven of the nine also multiply the layer's opacity envelope into their output, so all seven were rendering at a flat full strength Pyre never gives them.

The two stateful simulations had the same stub in their own form: both passed a hardcoded full opacity where Pyre passes the layer's Alpha envelope, so a hosted flame was still burning at full strength in the frames where Pyre's had already gone out - 863 lit pixels on the last frame against Pyre's none.

Those three things are now authored on the generator itself, with Pyre's own factory values as their defaults, read off a real Pyre layer rather than retyped so the two tools cannot drift apart. They appear as three controls above the generator's dials: Frames, Fill and Alpha - with Fill absent, not greyed, on a generator that carries its own ramps, exactly as Pyre's Shape section does it.

Two things worth knowing that are not defects. First, at Pyre's defaults the first and last frame of a hosted generator are empty, in Pyre as well as in Shaper - Pyre's factory Alpha envelope fades in from nothing and out to nothing. That is why each contact sheet shows one populated column. It is the default, not a fault, and the Alpha row on the card is what flattens it. Second, the nine generators still differ from Pyre by one part in 255, on partially transparent pixels only. That is Pyre's own compositor rounding rather than a hosting error - not one fully opaque pixel disagrees anywhere - and matching it would mean adding a rounding loss to make a number read 100%.

Still open: the two dedicated Fire and Fireball simulation generators sit at a slightly different simulation state from Pyre's, because their dial defaults have drifted from the values Pyre's own harness uses. They are the only two of the 27 that do not match. Pyre's Fire and Fireball hosted as a layer are byte-identical, so there is already an exact route to both.
