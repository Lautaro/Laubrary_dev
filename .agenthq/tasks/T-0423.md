## Description

**Owner's ask (2 of 6):** the analyser under the chain editor in the Klip Editor animates very badly. The owner cannot tell whether it is the update rate or whether the output itself is wrong, and says Zounds should be able to update fast enough for proper UI animation.

**Carried over from:** T-0420 item 2. **Depends on / overlaps:** T-0421, which has the leading explanation for the rate half: the sound editor only asked for a redraw from inside its own drawing, so without focus it barely redrew. A fix for that is in the working tree, uncommitted, not yet seen working.

**The two halves to separate, by measurement not reasoning:**
- Rate: how often does the window actually redraw, and how often is the analyser's measurement refreshed? (The measurement was deliberately made to happen on a pause rather than every frame; that alone reads as steppy.)
- Values: when it does update, is what it shows right? Compare against a known input.

**Done means:** the owner can watch it move smoothly in the real window, with and without focus, and the values are shown to be right against a known signal.

### 2026-09-27T13:30:38Z — agent — in_progress -> in_progress
Findings so far, from reading the code and then confirming by measurement:
1. The combined view is not live. It renders 1.5 s of test noise through the chain, reads 7 moments 171 ms apart, and loops those 7 pictures. That is about 3 frames per second at best, so it can only ever look steppy.
2. It plays those 7 pictures at half speed: each is held for 341 ms (the length of one reading) instead of 171 ms (the spacing between readings).
3. Once a measurement has finished, it stops asking to be redrawn at all. The animation only advances when something else redraws the window, such as the mouse moving over it. This is independent of T-0421 (the sound editor not redrawing without focus) and makes it worse.
4. Its 1.5 s has nothing to do with the sound: a 'strength over the play' curve is stretched across the 1.5 s test rather than the sound's real length, so what it shows over time does not line up with what is heard.
The values themselves (how much each band is changed at a given moment) come from a careful, stable measurement, and I have found no fault in them. The problem is WHEN and HOW OFTEN they are shown.
Plan: measure over the sound's own play, keep redrawing while it animates, move smoothly between readings instead of jumping, and when the sound is playing, follow its real play position so the picture matches what is heard.

### 2026-09-27T14:22:37Z — agent — in_progress -> done
Done, both halves.
RATE (the main problem): the time view now (a) covers the sound's real play instead of a fixed 1.5 s, (b) while the sound plays, shows the moment actually being heard, otherwise loops the play at real speed with a short pause, (c) moves smoothly between readings instead of jumping, (d) keeps asking to be redrawn while anything moves (it used to stop after every measurement), and (e) redraws without focus (T-0421).
VALUES: a still effect reads perfectly still: 0.000 dB of movement over 16 readings, for a low-pass and for a distortion. Against a case with a known answer (a cutoff wobbled at up to 10 Hz but kept far above the bass, where nothing below 1 kHz should change), every bass band read within 0.12 dB of unchanged. One real flaw was found and fixed: under a fast modulator the bars came out jagged, because a modulation shifts energy a few hertz sideways and the old reading counted that as lost. Bands are now read as energy in over energy out, which halves that jaggedness while keeping still effects still.
NEW: above the bars, one line per modulated parameter, read from the engine at its own update step, showing where it actually goes across the play, with a playhead and the playing sound's live value. It is shown in every analyser view, so switching tabs no longer changes the panel's height.
In the owner's current chain, the big bass bars that move as the LFO runs are real: the resonant cutoff sweeps down through the bass, and a resonance passing through a band really does lift it for a moment.

- Verified by eye: the loop running at real speed and wrapping; 'following the sound as it plays' while it plays; the lanes with playhead and live dot; the same panel height in Combined and Spectrum.
