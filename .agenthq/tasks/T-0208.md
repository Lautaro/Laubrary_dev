A chunk recipe is no longer a debris burst with seven optional extras bolted to it. It is an ordered list of capabilities, and a recipe holds exactly the ones it uses - any number of them, in any order.

Nine kinds ship. Four put something on screen: a Debris Scatter (the shrapnel a recipe has always thrown), a Fragment Fracture (a picture coming apart into a few large pieces), a Palette Splash (a spray of the exploding thing's own colours) and a Pyre Blast (one blast, or a line or a ring of them). Three decorate what a producer made: a Trajectory (blasts that fly instead of playing where they landed), a Trail (a puff left behind on a timer) and Hits (debris that can actually hurt what it touches). Two coordinate: a Layer Plan (the named depth slots the pieces sit in) and Cues (the sounds and named game-code moments along the way).

What this buys, concretely: a recipe can now hold three blasts at three different instants, or two kinds of debris on two different beats, or a trail on the fragments and a different one on the debris. None of that was expressible before - a slot existed once or not at all, and only the slots that happened to have names could be scheduled.

Everything sits on ONE clock, and a capability's moment is stated as seconds from the start of the recipe rather than as an offset from something else. Overlap is therefore the normal case, not something you have to trick the schedule into. How long the whole recipe lasts now has a single owner, which fixes a real defect: the burst used to size its own lifetime one way while the schedule reported another, so a blast placed after the debris had died could be cancelled before it ever appeared, with nothing said. The window's transport will scrub over exactly the number the runtime tears down by.

Three things behave differently and are worth a look after syncing:
- Anything not put in a named depth slot now draws in the order you authored it in, rather than every piece landing on the emitter's one number. A recipe with several producers and no Layer Plan may change which piece is in front.
- A recipe's Direction stays a property of the composition, not of its debris - every producer that follows the burst direction turns together. Each producer still owns how WIDE its own cone is.
- A pinned seed still reproduces exactly, now on every platform and forever, because the randomness was moved onto a generator written out in the tool rather than borrowed from the runtime. The arrangement a given seed produces is not the one the old seed gave. Recipes left unseeded still reroll every play, unchanged.

Every existing recipe upgrades itself once. All seven in this project were upgraded and re-saved: the flagship 'Floating Disc Blowup' came out as five capabilities (its layer plan, its fracture at 0.08s, and its three blasts at 0s / 0.26s / 0.46s, each keeping its own depth slot), and the six plain debris recipes as one capability each. Their old values are still on the file, read by nothing but that upgrade, and come out one release from now. Expect your ChunkSpec assets to show as modified the first time you open the project after syncing - that is the upgrade being written down.

Nothing a game calls changed: the three ways to throw a burst have the same shape and the same behaviour they had.

One thing is deliberately missing: the Chunks window's body is empty. It browses, creates, renames, duplicates and tags recipes as before, but there are no dials in it until it is rebuilt around the stack. An empty panel is honest; dials editing fields nothing reads are not.

=== DECISIONS MADE WHERE SPEC AND SOURCE DISAGREED ===
1. Modifiers report OccupiesTime = false. Section 2 only says "false for coordinators", but a modifier has no independent moment - its timing is its producer's - so giving it a lane would offer a Delay dial that changes nothing. Consequence for W2: Trajectory/Trail/Hits cards show NO Delay dial and get NO timing lane.
2. Cues is both a capability (a card in the stack, per section 3) and "one list on the spec" (per section 2): the list lives on the Cues capability and the spec reaches it through the stack. Only one Cues capability is meaningful; the Add menu should not offer a second.
3. PyreBlast's "inline formation" is a nested SpawnFormation plus a Pattern PROPERTY over useFormation + formation.shape, rather than flat fields. This keeps SpawnFormation as the single implementation of the placement maths (the editor preview must call the same Resolve) and makes migration a direct copy. THE CARD MUST WRITE Pattern, never useFormation or formation.shape separately.
4. PyreBlast.blastSeconds is a new authored dial. IChunkEffectSpawner deliberately exposes no duration, so Chunks cannot ask a blast how long it plays. It sizes the clock ONLY and never cuts playback. If Pyre later grows a duration hook this dial should go.
5. Randomness: System.Random and UnityEngine.Random are out of every roll. Each capability takes ONE draw to seed a ChunkRng (Capabilities/ChunkRng.cs - mixed seed + xorshift32) when its seed is 0, and uses the authored seed otherwise. Visual consequence: a seeded recipe's arrangement changes once, then is stable forever and identical across platforms. Section 8.9's hash(seed, index) pool pick is exactly what PickSpawner now does.
6. Default layerName on every producer is "" (not "Debris"/"Blast"/...), so an unslotted capability takes the flat fallback per section 8.6. The fallback is sortingOrder + stackIndex - literally "the same flat sorting fallback ChunkModuleContext uses today", plus stack order. NOTE: section 8.6 also says unslotted draws BEHIND every slotted output; that is not enforced, because LayerSpec.baseOrder (default 0) and the emitter's sortingOrder (default 500) are independent numbers the author owns. If "always behind" is required, it needs a LayerSpec change and section 8.10 forbids one.
7. Trail and Hits accept FragmentFracture as a target (section 8.8) - which is new runtime behaviour, since the old model could only trail/arm plain debris. Implemented rather than left as a picker entry that does nothing.
8. PaletteSplash gained its own pixelsPerUnit. It used to read the spec's, which no longer exists as a live field.
9. A brand-new ChunkSpec is born with one Debris Scatter: schemaVersion's initialiser must be 0 (Unity keeps a field initialiser for a field absent from old YAML, so any other default would strand every existing asset), which means a fresh instance runs the upgrade over its own legacy defaults. That default is the right one anyway - a recipe has always meant a debris burst - but W2 should know that "New" is not an empty stack.
