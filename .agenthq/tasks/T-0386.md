## Description

Follow-up to T-0385 (`MirageRig.cs`'s inverted PPU normalization, now fixed at commit `7986791a`). Five assets have an `imageZoom` hand-tuned against the OLD, broken formula and will now render their backdrop image roughly 39x too large:
- `Assets/Mirage/MirageDemo.asset`
- `Assets/Mirage/Test 2.asset`
- Three `BackSplash/*.asset` catalog entries (find them — T-0385's agent identified them during its visual pass, check its handover for the exact paths if not obvious from a search for `imageZoom` values well above 1 combined with a 100-PPU-imported backdrop image)

**Owner's explicit guidance (2026-09-21), read carefully — this is NOT "just restore the old look":** *"Retune the zoom. It's always been way too zoomed in and rarely the opposite."* This means: do NOT simply back-calculate the mathematically-neutral `imageZoom` that exactly reproduces the OLD (pre-fix) on-screen appearance and stop there — that would faithfully preserve a look the owner just said has chronically been too zoomed in. Instead:

1. For each of the 5 assets, compute the mathematically-neutral `imageZoom` first (the value that exactly cancels out T-0385's ratio flip and reproduces the old apparent size) — call this the baseline, and show your work (old zoom, old ratio, new ratio, neutral zoom) in the handover so it's checkable.
2. Then apply a further, clearly-flagged reduction from that baseline as a first-pass correction for the "always too zoomed in" pattern — something in the range of half the neutral value is a reasonable starting guess, but use your own judgment per-asset if one clearly reads differently once you can see it (e.g. a backdrop that's ALREADY appropriately framed at the neutral value shouldn't be needlessly shrunk further — the owner said "rarely the opposite," meaning this is a general tendency to correct for, not a blanket formula to apply blindly to every asset without looking).
3. Record BOTH numbers (neutral baseline and what you actually set) per asset in the handover, so the owner can easily dial any individual one back toward the neutral baseline if your first-pass guess overshoots.
4. Screenshot each of the 5 backdrops before/after (in Mirage or wherever they're actually visible) so the PM can review the visual result without needing to open the editor.
