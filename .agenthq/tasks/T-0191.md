Every generator you host in Shaper keeps its colour now.

The complaint was exact and it was engine-wide: Pyre's Gem paints 608 distinct colours in Pyre's own window and painted exactly ONE inside a Shaper document. Arc Burst, 268 against one. The cause was not the Pyre bridge and not a regression - Shaper had been baking each generator's whole picture and reading only its alpha since the escape hatch was built. The colour half was written into ShaperCompiledComposite.pixels (Runtime/Shaper/ShaperCompiler.cs:962) and no line in the package ever read it, so what you saw was the default flat fill painted over the generator's silhouette. The field's own comment said what it was for; only the consumer was missing.

It is there now. A composite layer's picture IS its albedo. The same document paints Gem's 608 and Arc Burst's 268, on the same pixels it covered before, and the side-by-side of Pyre's own render against Shaper's is indistinguishable.

Two rules come with it, and they are what make it right rather than merely colourful. The picture is not lit again: your Gem arrives with its facets lit, its edge lines drawn and its two glows applied, all computed by Pyre's own light model, and putting Shaper's rig over that would shade a picture of a lit object as though it were flat paint. Shading a composite is the generator's job - Pyre's Light card is right there on the layer. And it carries no height, because a generator publishes a picture and nothing else; your layer's own height stage is untouched.

A composite used as a member of a bag is deliberately unchanged: it still contributes its silhouette and no colour, so it cannot tint the bag that fused it. The middle panel of each attached strip is exactly that case, still flat, on purpose.

Your three notes from this afternoon are in the same commit.

The Half extent X/Y and Bake W/H dials are gone. You were right that they scale the disc and make no sense next to Pyre's Size: they were a second size authority over one number, with nothing on screen to tell you which of the two you were turning. The box is fitted to the canvas by the renderer now, one bake texel per canvas pixel, which is also the only ratio that carries a generator's colours across without quietly resampling them.

The Fill and Border sections are gone from a composite node - absent, not greyed. A greyed card still promises something you could reach; the truth is that neither applies. Your colour is authored on the generator, and a border traced on a composite has nothing real to trace: a composite's field is a coverage raster inverted into a pseudo-distance that is only meaningful within about a texel of its own edge, so a strip on it can only draw the wrong band.

That last one turned up a bug worth knowing about, because it was eating your colour even after the fix. ShaperNode.border is a plain serializable field and Unity never writes null for one, so EVERY composite saved into a document came back carrying a default border object nobody authored - and that ghost strip painted the default fill straight over the generator's picture. A 608-colour Gem loaded from an asset came back as 51. The resolver refuses a border on a composite structurally now, so the ghost cannot bind.

And the ramp works: on Pyre > Disc, changing the hosted card's own Fill from the warm default to blue repaints the disc, the filmstrip and the card's swatch in 43 ms.

The demo sheet was rebaked in place - same file, same GUID, same 768x192 layout, the same 31,325 covered pixels - and its ArcBurst layer went from 943 distinct colours to 1,835.

One thing I did NOT do, and you should know why. I do not touch the demo's ShaperDemoDoc.asset. Saving that asset from a script destroys managed references: measured on two duplicates, an ordinary SaveAssets nulled 40 of its 180 SerializeReference entries whether or not Undo was involved, and did not even persist the field I was changing. I hit it once, reverted it immediately, and worked only on the baked PNG bytes afterwards. Anything that needs editing inside that document has to be done by hand in the window.

**Technical details:**
COMMIT c4aab145 on feat/shaper (staged by path; the tree's pre-existing Samples~ / _Recovery / Assets.Editor noise was left alone).

NEW in Runtime/Shaper/ShaperCompositeDef.cs
  - ShaperCompositeAlbedo: the binding (raster + the CompositeSample op's canvas->local matrix + half-extents),
    lifted out of a fill owner's compiled program ONCE at bind time via ShaperCompositeAlbedo.From(program).
    From() returns null unless the program holds exactly one CompositeSample op and exactly one baked picture -
    which is a lone Composite node, and equally a NATIVE swarm (EmitCompositeSwarmNative bakes the whole swarm
    into one raster, which is genuinely the picture to paint with). A generic swarm fans out to N rasters with no
    single picture, so it binds nothing and keeps the flat fill rather than picking one member's colours for all.
  - ShaperCompositeDef.FitTo(w,h) / FitTree(node,w,h): halfExtent = canvas/2, bake = canvas, 1 texel per pixel.
  - pixels' doc corrected: the raster is STRAIGHT alpha, not premultiplied (the task brief assumed premultiplied).
    Evidence: PyreRenderer.Over un-premultiplies after every blend (ShaperEffectStageRunner.cs:108-109 states the
    same law), PyreSupersample.cs:58 un-premultiplies the block average, PyreJetEngine.cs:719 says "straight alpha".

EDIT Runtime/Shaper/ShaperEvaluator.cs
  - FillCompositeAlbedoTile(src, grid, x0,y0,w,h, albedo, dstOffset, dstStride): writes straight LINEAR rgb.
  - SampleCompositeColour: bilinear in PREMULTIPLIED space then un-premultiplied (a straight-alpha lerp across an
    edge texel mixes in a transparent texel's colour - a one-texel halo). Same clamp box as SampleCompositeCoverage
    so colour and coverage agree outside the box too. Fully transparent neighbourhood -> nearest texel, never a
    divide by zero. RGB through ShaperSrgb.DecodeChannel, alpha NOT decoded - the same split ShaperFillCompiler
    makes for a Texture fill's pixels (ShaperFillCompiler.cs:630-635).

EDIT Runtime/Shaper/ShaperFillResolver.cs
  - ShaperFillOwner.compositeAlbedo (null on every other owner; the pre-T-0191 path is taken literally when null).
  - Walk(): binds it whenever a Composite node became a fill owner. UNCONDITIONAL - a composite has no Shaper fill
    to lose to, since the window offers no Fill card on one. Clause 2 needs no code: FC-3.2 gives a default fill to
    the layer ROOT only, so a nested composite never becomes an owner.
  - BindBorder(): returns -1 for a Composite node. Had to be in the ENGINE, not only the window: ShaperNode.border
    is a plain [Serializable] field, Unity's serializer never writes null for one, so every SAVED composite carried
    a default border. Measured on a freshly saved one-Gem asset: 2 owners, the ghost strip painting the FC-3.2
    default over the picture, 608 distinct colours down to 51.
  - PaintTile(): after ShaperFillOps.FillTile (the fill still runs - its VEIL gates this owner's coverage), the
    albedo is overwritten from the picture and heightDelta is cleared. doLight gains `ow.compositeAlbedo == null`,
    which overrides ShaperLightResponse.receiveLighting (default true, ShaperLightRig.cs:217) - honouring it would
    double-light every composite in the project by default.
