The Chunks preview shows the burst now, and it moves.

Where the stage used to be a backdrop with a bar sliding across it, it now draws the recipe itself at whatever instant the clock is on. Every producer draws its aim as an arrow and how wide it throws as a cone. A Pyre Blast draws one footprint per point of its pattern, each sized from the effect it actually spawns rather than from a generic circle, numbered by the order the stagger fires them in, appearing at its own moment and fading out over its own length. Debris, fragments and splash particles are dots on their real arcs: the same gravity, the same drag, the same upward kick, the same floor bounce the burst will use, so a path curves and slows on screen the way it will in the game, and each piece is a small square turned by its own spin so a spin dial is something you can see. A Trajectory draws the arc it flies its blasts along and carries them down it. A Trail leaves puffs behind at its own interval. Hits draws its radius round each piece it arms. Everything takes its colour from its Layer Plan slot and draws in that slot's depth order, so what is in front on the stage is what will be in front on screen; with no layer plan it falls back to the order the cards are stacked in.

Timing is what the whole thing is for, so nothing is drawn out of its moment. A capability whose delay has not come round yet is a faint outline - a promise of what is about to happen, in the right place, at the right size. Once its output is over it is gone rather than frozen on its last frame. Press Play and the clock runs at real speed; with Loop on it wraps and keeps going; the transport's handle, the readout and the timing playhead are one instant, so moving either one moves the other and the picture with it. Dragging a dial while it runs changes the very next frame: playback does not stop, stutter or jump back to the start, because the picture is worked out afresh every frame rather than rendered once and kept.

It holds still when it should. The same instant, drawn twice with an edit and its undo in between, comes back pixel for pixel identical - a recipe left on the default "reroll every play" seed previews a stable arrangement instead of reshuffling under your cursor while you tune it. The stage frames itself to how far the whole recipe ever throws things, counting the capabilities you have switched off, so flicking one off to look at the rest cannot rescale or shift what is left; and the picture's own size is decided by the resize bar alone, so adding or removing a capability never moves it.

Two things in the picture are approximations rather than the real answer, and both are the same kind of compromise: a fracture's pieces start on a ring round its source rather than on the exact seams it will cut, and a splash's particles scatter inside its source's bounds rather than on its exact opaque pixels. Both real answers need reading a texture, which is not something that can happen sixty times a second. What each piece then DOES - how fast, which way, how long it lives, how it falls - is exact in both cases.

One thing worth knowing about how it reads: the stage frames the whole flight, so a recipe whose pieces travel a long way late on (the demo's fragments reach about eight units) shows its first half-second small and central. The picture is honest about scale rather than zoomed to whatever is on screen right now, which would make everything appear to move whenever nothing did. Drag the preview taller if the early moments are what you are tuning.

THE SHELL HOOK I ADDED (the only edit outside my two files)
    internal void InvalidatePreview() { previewGeneration++; stage?.MarkDirtyRepaint(); }
`previewGeneration` is declared in ChunkWindow.Preview.cs. It is unavoidable: the brief requires the stage's spatial extent to be cached and recomputed on invalidate only, and InvalidatePreview is the only signal the window raises for "the recipe changed". Nothing else is keyed off it - the picture itself is rebuilt from live data every repaint.

ChunkPreviewSim API (internal, Editor assembly)

    float Reach(ChunkSpec spec, ChunkPreviewFrame scratch)   // samples Build at 25 instants across the clock, includeDisabled: true

ChunkPreviewFrame is three reused lists (Cones, Paths, Guides) plus Reach; ChunkGuide is {pos, radius, angleDeg, alpha, color, shape, order, label} in world units with the origin at (0,0), so the sim knows nothing about rects or GUI. Shapes: Square (an oriented chunk/fragment, so spin reads), Dot (splash particle, trail puff), Disc (a blast's footprint), Ring (a Hits radius, or a blast that has not fired yet).

WHAT EACH KIND DRAWS
- Debris Scatter: aim arrow + spread cone; one oriented square per chunk on its integrated arc (gravity, exp drag, upward bias, floor bounce/friction/rest), sized by sizeOverLife, faded by alphaOverLife, tinted by colorOverLife, turned by its own spin (or pointed along velocity when faceVelocity is on). Plus a Hits ring and Trail puffs when a modifier targets it.
- Fragment Fracture: aim arrow + cone; pieceCount squares launched by Fire's own LerpAngle blend between the aim and each piece's radial direction, off the same ChunkRng(seed, 1) stream, integrated with its own gravity/drag, faded by alphaOverLife, turned by its own spin. Pending state also draws a ring at the source's own half-extent.
- Palette Splash: half-spread cone (matching Fire, which halves it - Debris does not); a dot per particle at sizePx/ppu, on its own arc, faded by alphaOverLife.
- Pyre Blast: SpawnFormation.Resolve for the points (the seedOverride overload, so an unseeded formation previews stably without touching the asset); a disc per point appearing at delay + that point's own stagger and fading out linearly over blastSeconds; numbered by FIRING RANK, computed from the resolved delays, so ties (a FromCentre pair) share a number and changing Order renumbers the picture. Radius = half the world size of the first frame of the picked spawner, times a scale drawn from its own stream. Pool pick = hash(seed, point index), per design decision 9.
- Trajectory: for each of its target blast's points, the launch Trajectory.Apply computes (same ChunkRng(seed, index), same cone, same upward bias), integrated with its own gravity/drag and honouring untilTargetEnds vs lifeSeconds; drawn as a thinned polyline AND used to move that point's disc.
- Trail: puffs at every multiple of `interval` along the target piece's sampled path, freshest brightest, capped at 24 per piece.
- Hits: a ring of radiusScale x the piece's own size around each of the target producer's pieces.
- Layer Plan: no guide of its own; it decides every other guide's colour and depth.
- Cues: ticks on the stage's clock strip, white while |t - cue| < 0.1 s. Nothing on the picture.

DETERMINISM
Seed = the authored seed when non-zero, else an FNV-1a hash of the capability's own id. That substitution is forced: the runtime's "seed 0 = reroll every play" is a UnityEngine.Random draw, which BC-1.3 bans in preview code and which would reshuffle the picture on every repaint. ChunkSprites.Count is asked once (not hard-coded) because the runtime takes a draw against exactly that number, and a stale copy here would silently shift every later value in a seeded scatter.

CACHING
- Source sizes (a blast's spawner, a fracture's source) are measured once per Object and cached, because measuring means asking IChunkAnimation.GetFrames, which can be a real render. Cleared on every window rebuild.
- The stage's extent is cached against (spec instance, previewGeneration).
- Nothing else. The picture is recomputed every repaint on purpose - a cache is exactly the mechanism that shows a stale frame after a mid-play edit.

PERFORMANCE
Fixed 1/60 s integration steps (the runtime's own per-frame step; a closed-form parabola would visibly disagree the moment drag is non-zero), capped at 900 steps. 200 guides per capability, 600 per frame. Trajectory arcs are thinned to 48 points for drawing while the integration stays at full resolution. Cones, paths and guides live in three lists reused for the life of the window.

DEVIATIONS AND JUDGMENT CALLS
1. THE PLAYHEAD MOVED. T-0210's stage drew it as a full-height bar sweeping across the picture. Once the stage shows where things are in SPACE, a vertical line crossing it reads as an object in the scene. It is now a 12 px clock strip along the bottom edge, which also carries the cue ticks. Flag it if you want the bar back.
2. A cone at 180 degrees of spread is drawn as an outline ring, not a filled disc. Measured: filled, it buried every guide inside it under the one statement that constrains them least.
3. A blast's disc is a 30%-alpha fill with a bright rim, not a solid disc. Measured on the demo: solid, the later blasts hid the earlier ones and the fragments entirely, which is the exact opposite of what a composition preview is for.
4. Layer colour: with a Layer Plan, colour comes from the slot index and an unslotted capability is grey; with NO Layer Plan at all, colour comes from the capability's place among the timed ones, using the SAME six colours the Timing lanes use, so a lane and the thing it stands for read as one object. Colouring everything grey when no plan exists would have been the literal reading of the spec and unreadable in practice.
5. Draw order for unslotted output. Design decision 6 says unslotted draws BEHIND every slotted output; T-0208 recorded that the RUNTIME does not enforce that (its numbers are baseOrder + index*step vs the emitter's sortingOrder, two independent values the author owns) and behaviour checklist 16.4 flags it for you. The preview follows the DESIGN's statement: unslotted behind, slotted by layer index. If the runtime is the truth here, the preview will disagree with a burst that mixes slotted and unslotted producers.
6. The scale is honest, not adaptive. Framing is to the whole recipe's reach across its whole clock, computed once and cached, counting switched-off capabilities. This is the spec's rule and it is what keeps the picture still, but on a recipe whose late flight is far larger than its early action (the demo: reach 7.87 units, mostly fragments at t=2) the first half-second reads small.
7. Fracture and splash emission points are approximations (see the details bucket). The class comment in ChunkPreviewSim states both, with the reason.
