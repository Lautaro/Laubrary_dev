Playback was never broken. It had no way to say what it was doing, and under cherry framing that is indistinguishable from a stopped transport.

The Frame slider is a SOURCE-frame index. Under cherry framing the playhead is not a frame at all, it is a (slot, beat) pair, and the slider only ever shows whichever source frame the sequence happens to name. On ShaperDemoDoc as shipped -- cherry on, two slots out of sixteen frames, a 0.15s loop gap -- that means the slider visits two of its sixteen positions and the picture goes genuinely blank between passes. Measured over 40 playback beats: 2 distinct transport positions, blank preview on 17 of 40. That is exactly "transport did not move, preview turned empty", produced by a document behaving as authored.

The blank is a real authored state (ShaperCherry.BlankFrame, Runtime/Shaper/ShaperCherry.cs:127; honoured at ShaperPreviewStage.cs:397-407), so the fix names the second meaning rather than removing it. A reserved single-line status under the preview now reports which cherry slot is showing and the source frame it resolved to, "Cherry gap" during the loop blank, and -- before Play is even pressed -- that cherry framing will follow N slots instead of the frame order. A cherry-enabled document with no slots at all, which the engine renders as a permanent blank forever, now says so instead of presenting a dead Play button. The same line covers the other way Play can look frozen: while the pre-baker has not reached the next frame, PlaybackTick deliberately holds the current one (ShaperWindow.cs:878-888), and the line reports it as caching rather than leaving a frozen picture unexplained.

Substituting frame 0 for a blank beat, or falling back to the plain frame order when a sequence is empty, would have traded a confusing preview for a lying one -- both are states the author deliberately created. The demo document keeps its cherry framing for the same reason: T-0179 authored it on purpose and the committed sheet, ShaperClip and AnimationClip beside it were baked in cherry order, so switching it off would desync four shipped bakes to fix a legibility problem that belongs in the transport. You may still want the demo to open on plain playback -- that is a demo-content call, not a bug, and it needs a rebake to stay consistent.

One genuine blanking bug was found and fixed on the way. ShaperPreviewStage.Refresh destroyed the texture the preview was displaying BEFORE it had the pixels to replace it with, then returned early when the frame had not rendered -- so a canvas resize racing an uncached frame left the picture empty on exactly the path where the last good frame should have been held. Pixels are now obtained first; the texture is only replaced once there is something to put in it.

SMELL ASSESSMENT -- keep the fix, and note the structural weakness behind it. This was not a local slip. The transport has one control (the Frame slider) carrying two incompatible clocks, and the cherry clock has no representation at all: no cherry-scoped scrubber, no slot position, no indication playback is even running. A status line names the second clock honestly but does not give it a control. If cherry framing is meant to be a first-class authoring mode rather than a checkbox, the real answer is a second scrubber over the SLOT sequence while cherry is on, with the frame slider going read-only -- which is a design decision, not a bug fix, so it is flagged rather than done.
