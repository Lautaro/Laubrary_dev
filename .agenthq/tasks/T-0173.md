Shaper can make fire now. It could compose almost anything else — nine hosted generators, solids, sprites, swarms — but not a flame, because the two flames Pyre owns are not the kind of thing the composite bridge could host. Fire (a fluid simulation carrying heat and fuel through a velocity field) and Fireball (the cellular doom-fire folded into kaleidoscope wedges) are ShapeForm enum cases driven by harnesses buried inside PyreRenderer, not PyreForm plug-ins, so there was no object to hand across and the whole family was simply absent from the tool.

Both are now generators an author picks from the Generator card, under a new Simulations section beside the nine forms. They drive Pyre's own FireSim and FireballSim untouched — no physics was re-ported, and Pyre needed no edit at all, because the assembly those sims live in was already referenced by the bridge; the only missing half was the one that speaks Shaper.

What this buys an author is that a flame is finally on the same clock as everything else. Its burn is an envelope over the document's phase, and every rate — heat, buoyancy, curl, reach, the lot — can be a curve, a seeded draw or a step sequence read at each frame's own phase. A candle swells into a blaze and dies back as one authored shape rather than as a timeline of tweaks. That is only possible because a flame is reached rather than evaluated: it is genuinely stateful, so each frame is replayed from a reset, and the replay is cached on a content hash. Playing or baking forward costs one simulation step per frame; touching any dial forces a cold replay, because changing a dial changes every earlier frame too and reusing a checkpoint would quietly show a frame built under the old values.

Both publish coverage the ordinary way — the ramp's transparent end is the silhouette — so fill, border, height, lights and effects all apply to a flame knowing nothing about where it came from.

Two limits worth stating rather than discovering. Emitters are the built-in fixed ones: a composite source is handed only size, phase and seed, so it cannot see the node it hangs on or that node's swarm; Shaper's swarm still instances the finished flame, but it cannot feed the flame's interior the way Pyre's swarm-driven emitters do, and closing that gap means widening the composite contract itself. And Heat is not published as a fill quantity: the composite contract exposes exactly one entry point, Render, and coverage is derived from its alpha — publishing a second quantity is an engine change to the contract, not something a source can opt into today.
