**Details:**
How to use this: press Play on the ProtoGuy demo scene and fire in different directions -- every shot leaves a line, and they fan out into a smooth fan rather than a fixed number of spokes. "Clear Fire Lines" in the top-left wipes them. To go back to either of the other two ways of aiming, select the ProtoGuySpawner object in the scene and swap its character field to "ProtoGuy EvenAim" (the even sixteen) or "ProtoGuy" (the hand-drawn one).

Four dials on the character (Zoes window, AI section, "Aiming"): how many directions to round the aim to (0 = none, fully free -- try 32 or 64 if free feels too loose); the furthest the drawing may be turned (0 = no limit; lowering it keeps the picture honest but then the bullet stops following the barrel); a step to snap the turn to (stops slow turns shimmering, at the cost of the barrel lagging slightly); and the point the upper body swivels around.

Verified by probe: sixty-four aim angles spread evenly around the circle were fired through the real firing path -- every single shot went exactly where it was aimed, to within zero degrees, so aiming really is continuous. The upper body is never turned more than 11.25 degrees (half the gap between drawn poses), the legs are never turned at all, and the muzzle the shot comes out of is the hand-drawn one carried around by the same turn, checked against the arithmetic by hand. Both other characters were spawned again afterwards and each still uses its own aiming, unchanged. No errors or new warnings.

Verified by eye (screenshots attached): the even fan of shots at 32 and at 64 directions; a close-up facing right, where the body is not turned at all and the line leaves the barrel tip perfectly; the same pose 11.25 degrees off, where the upper body is visibly tilted and the shot still leaves the barrel; and a heavily zoomed-in shot of that tilted body.

Where it looks bad -- honestly:
1. Turned pixels. At the largest turn the upper body's pixels no longer line up with the legs' or the background's, so its edges go slightly ragged and one row of pixels can look taller than the next. At normal viewing size it is mild and reads as a lean; zoomed right in (attached) it is obvious. This is inherent to turning pixel art and cannot be fixed, only limited.
2. The barrel does not always point where the bullet goes. Not because of the turning -- because the hand-drawn muzzle arrows on some of the sixteen poses are themselves off by up to 18 degrees from the direction that pose is supposed to face (average 4.3). The shot is always exactly right; on those poses the drawn gun is the thing that is wrong, and this technique makes that visible where the old one hid it. Repainting those few arrows would fix it.
3. The waist. Because the upper body swivels exactly on the point where it is joined to the legs, no gap ever opens -- the seam holds. What it costs instead is that a character facing towards or away from the camera reads as leaning to one side rather than turning.
4. Nothing jumps when the closest drawn pose changes over: the drawing swaps but the direction it depicts stays smooth, so the change is only a change of artwork, not a jerk.

Not yet verified: nobody has driven this by hand with a mouse or gamepad -- every test drove the aim from a script. The hand pass is still yours. The three extra dials are wired and saved but were left at their defaults throughout, so their effect has not been eyeballed.

How the facing is reused rather than duplicated: ProtoGuy's UpperAim16 is a DirectionMode.Rotation sheet (16 frames = 16 directions, resolved by LauminationSetResolver.ResolveRotationSheet), NOT a Members set -- so a hand-written "22.5 deg table" in the aimer would have been a second copy that breaks the moment the sheet is re-baked at 32. MotionPoseAnimator already computes the answer; IResolvedFacing just publishes it. residual = Mathf.DeltaAngle(resolvedFacing, aimAngle). Mirroring needs no special case: LauminationSetResolver.NearestMember already returns 360-angle as ResolvedAngleDeg for a member picked through a built-in mirror, i.e. the effective world facing, and flipX is applied in sprite space before the transform rotation.

Why it works without fighting anything: the weapon slot is parented under the part named by ZoeWeaponSlot.attachToPartName (Upper), so GetComponentInParent<IResolvedFacing>() lands on exactly the torso to turn -- no part name plumbed through IAimSpec.Attach. ProtoGuy's Upper attaches to Legs with parentAnchor = Pivot + (0, 1.3125) and childAnchor = Pivot + zero, so CompositeZonedPlayer pins the Upper transform's ORIGIN there absolutely every LateUpdate, independent of its rotation -- the rotation pivot is therefore already the hip joint and the stitcher and the rotation never fight. DefaultExecutionOrder(100) puts the aimer after MotionPoseAnimator and CompositeZonedPlayer (both order 0); the rotation written is absolute (-(baseRotationDeg + residual)), never a delta, so a frame where the animator early-returns cannot accumulate. pivotOffset (default zero, so normally no position write at all) is skipped when the part transform IS the owner transform, or a single-body Zoe would be driven around the screen.

Muzzle: the aimer samples IAnimatedView.TryGetMetaVectorNearest(muzzleLayerId) AFTER writing the rotation. ZonedAnimationPlayer.PixelToWorld/DirectionToWorld go through that same transform, so the painted origin comes back already rotated -- verified numerically (aim 11.25: measured muzzle (0.4460, 1.7926) vs hand-rotating the N-frame muzzle about (0, 1.3125) = (0.446, 1.792)).

Measurements (attached sweep64.tsv, 64 angles; sweep.tsv is the same at 32). Method: AimDriver disabled, Combatant.aimDirection driven from an EditorApplication.update state machine, ProjectileWeapon.TryFire() through the real ResolvedAimDirection() path, and the fired direction read off the Projectile's private dir field via reflection (the T-0247 gotcha -- re-asking ResolvedAimDirection() in the Fired handler is not authoritative). max |shot - aim| = 0.0000 deg over 64/64; max |residual| = 11.25 deg; painted barrel vs aim ranges -11.61 .. +18.03 deg, mean |4.29| -- entirely per-frame authoring error in the Muzzle vector layer (worst at 202.5 deg / SSW, exactly 18.0 deg off with zero rotation applied), not introduced here.

Sprite facts for the pixel-rotation question: filterMode Point, PPU 16, atlas 1825x34. Rotation is world-space at sub-pixel resolution, so no filtering blur -- the artifact is re-sampling stagger on the point-filtered edges, not blur.

Left as-is deliberately: FireDirectionVisualizer stays enabled; ProtoGuy.asset and ProtoGuy EvenAim.asset untouched (both re-spawned in Play and confirmed still attaching MuzzleVectorTracker and EvenDirectionsAimer respectively, both with the torso at rotation 0).
