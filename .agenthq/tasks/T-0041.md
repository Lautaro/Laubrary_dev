## Description

This is a RnD planning task. Post the answer in planning tab as an MD. Some very heavy needs for solutions that touches most parts of the game and changes the game type and perspective.

Examples:
3d racing. Car stops at location. Makes a 90° slide turn and stops in a side pose. Driver gets out and walks across the screen to the right side. Screen starts to scroll to the right. Platforms and buildings are revealed. Game is now a side scrolling run and gun. 

Weve started looking at an Interlude system as well that can create cut scenes, intros, endings. Examples:

Start of stage. Interlude controls agents. Enemies walk in on stage. Player walk in. Text displays: Stage 1 get ready. Text animates out. Game starts.

Halfway through level interlude resumes control. Player moves to designated spot. Miniboss appears. Text: miniboss appears! Text fades out. Game resumes.

End of stage. Text: stage cleared. Player walks out of screen. 

Interlude is supposed to work with cartographer which are authored and procgen tile maps. So maybe it wont be practical to have interlude operate on 3d racing scenes. Although it seems that the transition from 3d to 2d like describe prev could be solved by imposing a 2d level on the 3d?

The research is done and written up as a plan you can read in the Planning tab under Cartographer. Short version: the dramatic 3D-racing-becomes-a-side-scroller moment is more achievable than it looks, because the pieces for it are mostly already built — but it should NOT be done by laying a 2D level over the 3D world, which is the one idea in the brief we recommend against. The cutscene system (Interlude) and that genre switch are two separate jobs that share one trick, and the plan says which parts to build in what order.
**Details:**
The plan answers your question about imposing a 2D level on the 3D scene with a clear no, and explains why: the two worlds measure space in incompatible ways, and forcing them together makes the level editor lie about what it is showing. The alternative is simpler — the two worlds sit side by side, both running, and only the PICTURE hands over. What has to match at the swap moment is what is on screen, not where things are in the world. Your instinct about the car doing a 90-degree slide into a side-on pose turns out to be the key to that, so it is kept.

Also worth knowing: your cutscene system does not need to understand 3D at all in order to drive that transition. It gives orders by name and the game carries them out. What it CAN'T do is let you author the 3D half visually — that stays a Cartographer-only editor, and the 3D part gets written as named commands with no preview. That is a real limitation, stated up front rather than discovered later.

Three things you need to decide before the biggest chunk of work can start; all six open questions are listed at the end of the plan, and none of them block the early items.

Not yet verified: two of the plan's recommendations are reasoned from how the existing code is shaped rather than proven by running anything — swapping a character between a 3D and a 2D body, and whether the camera cross-fade actually looks seamless. The plan flags both and puts a cheap prove-it-first step at the top of the work order for exactly that reason.

One correction worth surfacing: an earlier draft claimed the existing Interlude design document was unaware of a system called Arena. That was wrong and has been fixed — it discusses Arena at length. The genuine blind spot is a different system, Cabinets.
