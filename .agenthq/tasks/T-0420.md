## Description

### What this is

Four things the owner hit while using the chain editor for real, arriving while the crash in T-0419 was being tracked
down. Kept as one task because they arrived as one stream of use, and because two of them may turn out to be the same
underlying thing. Each is listed with its state so the board says which are actually settled.

### 1. The "Edited, re-measuring..." line — TO DO

Delete it outright. The owner's judgement is that a line of text saying something is recalculating is not worth the
space. If such a signal is ever genuinely needed, the right form is a small visual indicator rather than prose, and that
is recorded as the approach to take — but nothing is to be added now.

### 2. The analyser under the chain editor animates badly — TO DO

The owner cannot tell whether it is too slow to look like motion, or whether what it is showing is wrong, and says the
engine should be able to feed a display fast enough to animate properly. Both halves need distinguishing rather than
guessing: whether the redraw and re-measure rate is the problem, and whether the values themselves are right. Note that
the measurement was deliberately made to happen on a pause rather than every frame, which could read as steppy, and that
an earlier version of this display had a jitter fault that came from the measurement's own noise.

### 3. The modulated slider's overlay is not visible — TO DO

A thin marker was supposed to show where the user put a value while the coloured fill shows where a modulator has
actually taken it. It was reported as done and was checked at the level of the numbers being right; the owner is looking
at the screen and sees no change at all. So the numbers moving was never evidence that anything was drawn. Find out
whether the drawing is absent, present but invisible, or lost — and this time establish what is on screen, not what the
values are.

### 4. A modulator at 10 Hz appears to change SHAPE, not just speed — TO DO

The owner is right that it should not: rate should only change how often the same shape repeats. Three candidates, and
they are distinguishable rather than a matter of opinion: a display sampling too slowly for a fast oscillation (which
makes any shape look like a jagged approximation of itself), a genuine fault in how the oscillator steps its phase at
short periods, or the analyser's measurement window no longer holding when the thing it measures repeats every hundred
milliseconds. The underlying oscillator output has to be compared against the plain mathematical shape before deciding
which of the three it is.

### Standing note that came out of number 3

A check that proves the right numbers are being produced proves nothing about whether anything is visible. Anything with
a visual result gets looked at, or is reported as not verified by eye — never as done.
