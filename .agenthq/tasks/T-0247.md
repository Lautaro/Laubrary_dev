### 2026-09-09T09:59:43Z — agent — in_progress -> in_progress
ProtoGuy now has a second, alternative way of aiming. Instead of the shot direction coming from a line drawn by hand on each animation frame, it is worked out by maths: sixteen directions spread perfectly evenly around the circle (North, South, East, West and two steps between each), so the spread is exact rather than as good as the painting was. There is a second ProtoGuy character asset using it, the demo scene runs that one, and the tracing lines are switched back on so you can see the sixteen spokes straight away.

[[attach:20260909T095943Z_even-aim-16-spokes.png]] [[attach:20260909T095943Z_even-aim-cardinals-east-facing.png]] [[attach:20260909T095943Z_zoe-window-aiming-field.png]]
**Details:**
How to use this: press Play on the ProtoGuy demo scene and fire in different directions -- every shot leaves a line, and they fan out into an even sixteen-spoke star. "Clear Fire Lines" in the top-left wipes them.

To go back to the old aiming, select the ProtoGuySpawner object in the scene and swap its character field from "ProtoGuy EvenAim" back to "ProtoGuy". Nothing about the original ProtoGuy was changed.

To tune where the shot comes out, open the character in the Zoes window, switch the AI section on in the toggle bar, and open "Aiming". Four dials: how many directions; the point on the body the muzzle swings around; how far it swings out sideways and up/down (two numbers, because a top-down view squashes depth); and how far the gun sits to the shooter's right when facing towards or away from you. The numbers currently on the character were measured off the existing hand-drawn muzzle, so it lines up with the rifle.

Verified by probe: all sixteen directions come out exactly 22.5 degrees apart with zero error, even when the raw aim is deliberately fed in 7 degrees off the ring; the shot origins land within 0.02 of a world unit (well under one pixel) of where the hand-drawn muzzle puts them at North, South, East and West; the original ProtoGuy still spawns, still uses the old technique, still fires, and reproduces its previous numbers exactly; no errors in the console.

Verified by eye: the sixteen-spoke star in the game view; a close-up facing East where the line starts at the rifle's muzzle and runs straight along the barrel; the new Aiming control drawn in the Zoes window with its four dials.

Not verified: nobody has played this by hand with a gamepad or mouse -- the firing in the test was scripted. The twelve in-between directions were not eyeballed one by one against the drawn barrel; by design the origin is only approximate there, and only the four cardinals are promised to match.

**Technical details:**
THE SEAM, for T-0327 (third technique) to follow exactly.

A technique attaches a component implementing Laubrary.Combat2D.IVectorAimSource (already preferred by ProjectileWeapon.ResolvedAimDirection) and OWNS the muzzle Transform it is handed (the shot origin). owner.gameObject is the character root, so IAnimatedView / IPartLookup / the view are reachable from there -- that is how technique 3 gets at the torso sprite to rotate it. muzzleLayerId is passed to every technique, not just the painted one, precisely so technique 3 can read the authored muzzle metadata and rotate it with the sprite.

Where the choice lives: exactly one place -- Zoe.aiming, a [SerializeReference] IAimSpec on D:\UNITY\Laubrary Dev\Assets\Packages\Laubrary\Runtime\Zoetrope\Zoe.cs, attached per weapon slot by ZoeSpawner.EquipWeapon in D:\UNITY\Laubrary Dev\Assets\Packages\Laubrary\Runtime\Zoetrope\ZoeSpawner.cs, threaded SpawnCharacter -> EquipWeaponSlots -> EquipWeapon. ONE line does it: (aiming ?? DefaultAiming).Attach(shooter, muzzle, owner, muzzleLayerId). There is no if/else over technique anywhere -- not in ProjectileWeapon, not in WeaponMuzzleCue, not in the input drivers. Exactly one technique is attached per slot on purpose: two would fight over the same muzzle Transform every frame, and ProjectileWeapon consults only one IVectorAimSource.

Modules on the shelf today, both [System.Serializable], both peers:

Resolves in LateUpdate, same as MuzzleVectorTracker, and holds the last answer while aim is momentarily zero.

ORIGIN MODEL and where its numbers came from. Measured live in play mode off the existing MuzzleVectorTracker (muzzle world position minus character position) at each cardinal: E (1.2604, 0.9583), N (0.3438, 1.8704), W (-1.1701, 0.9769), S (-0.1701, 0.0833). Painted directions at those facings are 0.00 / 88.85 / -179.28 / -87.51 degrees -- i.e. today's spread is already up to ~1.5 degrees off even, which is the unevenness this task exists to remove. A rigid rotate-an-offset model does NOT fit that data (the lateral hold shows up in x at N/S but not in y at E/W, and forward reach is foreshortened vertically), so the model is: x = centre.x + reach.x*cos(theta) + side*sin(theta); y = centre.y + reach.y*sin(theta). Fitted values on the duplicate: centre (0.066, 0.972), reach (1.2153, 0.8936), side 0.257. Max residual against the four measured points is 0.021 world units, about 0.34 px at PPU 16. The class defaults are deliberately generic ((0,1), (1.2,0.9), 0.25), NOT ProtoGuy's -- the fitted numbers live on the asset.
