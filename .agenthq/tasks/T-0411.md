## Description

### What the owner reported

Verbatim: *"Modifier settings such as depth, offset ans [and] +×= often seems to produce values that makes little sense for what they are modifying. You need to come up with a clever solution that works for any modification target."*

The solution must generalise to **every parameter of all sixteen effects**, from their declared ranges and kinds — no per-effect special-casing.

### Diagnosis — why it happens

A modulator's output is combined with the parameter's authored value by one of three operations, and the amount is a raw multiplier applied to the modulator's own output before combining. The result is then clamped to the parameter's legal range. Three separate things go wrong, and they compound.

**1. "Depth" is expressed in the target's own raw units, so the same number means something different on every parameter.** A filter cutoff runs from 20 to 20000; a resonance runs from 0 to 1. To make a cutoff wobble audibly you need a depth in the thousands; the same musical wobble on resonance needs a depth of about a fifth. Nobody can guess the right number without knowing the parameter's internal range, which the interface never shows. This is most of what the owner is hitting.

**2. Multiply against an oscillator drives the parameter negative for half of every cycle.** An oscillator swings symmetrically about zero — it is a **bipolar** signal, roughly minus one to plus one. Multiplying a parameter by that makes it negative for half the cycle, where it is clamped to the parameter's minimum and simply sits there. Measured, not assumed: an oscillator on a 2000 Hz cutoff with the default settings produced a value that ranged from 1999.6 Hz down to exactly 20 Hz, the floor. That is not a sweep around the cutoff, it is a sweep that spends half its life pinned at the bottom — and it is presented to the user as the normal way to attach an oscillator.

**3. Modulators do not agree with each other about their output range.** An oscillator is bipolar (about −1 to +1); an envelope is unipolar (0 to 1); a step list emits whatever raw numbers were typed into it. So swapping one modulator for another on the same parameter, with the same depth, changes both the size AND the centre of the movement. The same authored depth is not comparable across modulator types either.

**4. Multiply is mathematically dead on any parameter whose resting value is zero**, which several effects have (mix and amount parameters commonly rest at zero). Multiplying zero by anything stays zero, so the modulator appears attached, reports a depth, and does nothing at all.

### Proposed solution — modulate along the control's own travel

Every parameter already declares a minimum, a maximum, and the kind of control it gets (linear, logarithmic, integer, toggle, or a choice list). That is exactly the information needed, and it exists for all sixteen effects already. Nothing per-effect has to be written.

**The rule: modulation happens in the same normalised zero-to-one space the slider itself uses, and depth is a fraction of the slider's travel.**

- Take the authored value and find where it sits along its own control, as a fraction from zero to one, using the *same* mapping the slider uses — logarithmic for a cutoff, linear for a mix.
- Normalise every modulator to one documented signal range, so an oscillator, an envelope, a random and a step list are interchangeable and comparable.
- Shift that fraction by the modulator's signal times the depth, and clamp the *fraction* to zero-to-one.
- Map the fraction back through the parameter's own curve, rounding where the parameter is an integer or a choice.

Why this answers all four problems at once:

- **Depth means one thing everywhere.** A depth of a quarter is a quarter of the slider's travel, whether that slider runs 20 to 20000 or 0 to 1. The number is finally guessable, and it transfers when a modulator is moved to a different parameter.
- **A logarithmic parameter sweeps by ratio, which is what makes a filter sweep sound even.** A fixed fraction of a logarithmic control is a fixed *ratio*, so the sweep sounds the same at the bottom of the range as at the top. An offset in raw units cannot do this — it is enormous at the bottom and inaudible at the top.
- **It cannot produce nonsense values**, because the clamp happens on the fraction before it is converted back, rather than letting a wild number be produced and then truncated. Nothing lands outside the legal range, and nothing gets stuck at a floor.
- **Zero resting values stop being a special case**, because shifting a position along a control works regardless of where that position is.

**The operator question follows from this.** Shifting along the control covers what Add and Multiply were both reaching for, correctly, in one operation. Multiply's only genuinely distinct meaning is proportional scaling of the value itself, which is meaningful on a few parameters and meaningless on the ones that rest at zero — so it should be offered only where it means something, rather than offered everywhere and quietly broken on half of them. Replace should land somewhere inside the legal range by construction, which the same mapping gives for free.

**The live value readout built in T-0410 is the other half of the answer.** Whatever the numbers say, the slider now shows where the engine has actually taken the value, so any combination is legible at a glance. The design above makes the numbers sensible; the readout makes them verifiable.

### Compatibility

This changes what an existing authored depth means, so existing chains need converting rather than reinterpreting. There is precedent in this project for a conversion applied on load plus an explicit action to make it permanent.

### Status — done, verified by measurement

Implemented as designed. A modulator now moves its parameter along that parameter's own control, and depth is a share of
that control's travel.

**What the check proves** (runnable from the Laubrary menu, kept):

- The SAME oscillator at the SAME depth attached to a filter cutoff and to a resonance — two parameters of the same
  effect whose raw spans differ by a factor of over seven thousand — sweeps **exactly 50.0% of each one's control**. That
  single number being identical on both is the whole fix.
- The cutoff sweep is centred on 2000.4 Hz when set to 2000, and the resonance sweep on 0.500 when set to 0.500. The old
  collapse, where half of every cycle sat pinned at the parameter's floor, is gone.
- A parameter with nothing attached reads exactly its authored value throughout the same render, which is what catches a
  "fix" that simply moves everything.

**A mistake worth keeping.** The check first reported the resonance sweep as off-centre. It was not: that control turns
out to be spaced by ratio as well, and the check had assumed it was evenly spaced, so it was measuring the centre the
wrong way. The subject was right and the test was wrong — the fix was to ask the parameter how it is spaced rather than
to assume.

**Old chains are translated, not reinterpreted.** A saved binding is marked as belonging to the older form and converted
whenever it is used; touching either of its controls rewrites it for good. Reinterpreting instead would have been silent
and wrong in both directions — a sweep authored as three thousand hertz read as a fraction would peg its parameter at
maximum forever.

**One old behaviour is deliberately not reproduced.** Multiplying by an oscillator drove parameters negative for half of
every cycle. There is no faithful translation of that, because reproducing it would mean reproducing the fault.

### Also fixed here: the oscillator's curve was being read as a waveform

The owner asked what the "free envelope" on an oscillator does, having taken it for a custom waveform option alongside
sine, triangle, saw and square. It is not one. It is a second, slower control that sets HOW STRONGLY the oscillation
applies as the sound plays — so a wobble can fade in, fade away or come and go — while the wave's shape stays whichever
of the four was chosen. It has a working editor (drag points, double-click to add) and starts flat, so it does nothing
until drawn on.

**The misreading was the interface's fault**: an unlabelled graph directly beneath a row of buttons named after waveforms
looks like where you draw your own. It now carries a visible caption, and each waveform button explains what it does to
the sound rather than only naming itself.
