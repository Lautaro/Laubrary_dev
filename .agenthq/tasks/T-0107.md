## Description

A border attaches to a shape node and traces that node's coverage - put it on a sub-shape to outline each blob, on the bag to outline the fused silhouette, on both for an outline plus interior dividers. The border produces a REGION (inward, outward or straddling, animatable width), not a colour; an ordinary fill paints it, which makes gradient, textured and animated outlines free. An outward strip joins the node's published coverage by default, with a switch to opt out. Much of this exists in today's Pyre already - the stage measures inward distance, paints the band with its own separate fill and animates the width - but it is gated to six of 29 generators and not even constructed in the UI for the other 23. Also decide what happens to the three generators that draw a private rim of their own (the lit solids, Text, Fire): port them as ordinary borders or keep the private rim and document why. Do not silently have both.

The authoritative design is the "Shaper - the design" tab of this group's planning document (also at D:\UNITY\Laubrary Dev\.agenthq\workspace\T-0098\SHAPER_THE_DESIGN.md). Read it before starting. Background: the three earlier reports are tabs of the PyrePlus group's planning document, and a condensed digest of them (incl. every withdrawn claim and every known live bug) is at D:\UNITY\Laubrary Dev\.agenthq\workspace\T-0098\P1-digest.md.

THE IDEA WORTH UNDERSTANDING. An outline is a REGION, not a colour. The stage works out which pixels are "the strip" and then hands that strip to the ordinary painting stage, exactly as if it were a small shape of its own. That single decision is why gradient, textured and animated outlines needed no new work at all, and it is why a future paint style will automatically work on outlines too without anyone thinking about it.

WHAT THE OLD TOOL GOT WRONG THAT THIS CANNOT. The old outline was measured from the picture it had already drawn, which meant it could only ever go inwards, it drifted and faded on any soft-edged shape, and it silently disappeared wherever a shape ran off the edge of the frame. This one is measured from the shape's own geometry instead, so outward outlines are free, the strip lands exactly where the edge is, and there is nothing to fall off.

THE QUESTION YOU ASKED ABOUT THE THREE ODD ONES OUT - answered, and the question itself had a mistake in it. You asked what to do about three things that draw their own private rim: the lit solids, Text, and Fire. Fire does not draw a rim at all; three separate fire routines were read end to end and none of them has one. The one that does is Inferno, and what it draws is not a rim either - it adds HEAT, and its colour comes entirely from the shape's own colour ramp. So: the lit solids keep theirs, because it draws the creases WITHIN a solid rather than its outside edge and no outline stage of any design could produce those; Text loses its private one and uses the public stage, which makes it better in four ways; and Inferno keeps its, because it is not an outline and never was. In two of the three cases the whole confusion was a naming problem - three different things were all called a rim.

FOUR FAULTS WERE FOUND BEHIND A FULLY GREEN TEST REPORT, which is now the third time running on this project. Two were real bugs. One was a safety margin that was too small on any squashed or stretched shape, letting the outline get cut off. The other made an outline's front-to-back order depend on a completely unrelated setting, so an outline could jump in front of something that should have covered it. Both fixed and measured before and after. Separately, four of the tests turned out to be incapable of ever reporting a failure; all four were rebuilt and then deliberately broken to prove they now can.

ONE OLDER FAULT FOUND AND DELIBERATELY LEFT ALONE. The same measurement turned up a margin that is too small on softly-blended shapes even with no outline involved at all. It belongs to the earlier shape stage, not to this one, and fixing it properly needs its own working-out rather than a patch smuggled in here. The numbers are in the verification report.

WAITING ON YOUR NOD - one thing, not blocking. There is a switch for "draw the outline but do not let it count as part of the shape". On a top-level shape it does exactly that. On a shape nested inside a group it makes the outline disappear entirely, because the group was never told the outline was there and so trims it off. That is the honest consequence of two rules you can see written down, and cell 08 of the picture is what it looks like - but if you would rather it stayed visible, that is your call to make and it is a small change.

THE CONTRACT is D:\UNITY\Laubrary Dev\.agenthq\workspace\T-0107\BORDER-CONTRACT.md. The four clauses that carry the most weight:
BD-1.2 - the strip is derived from the node's SIGNED DISTANCE, not its coverage. This contradicts the wording of SHAPER_THE_DESIGN.md:76 and :333 ("traces that node's coverage"). Behaviour is what those clauses describe; the mechanism is not. Coverage is identically zero outside the silhouette, so an outward strip is unrecoverable from it - which is exactly why Pyre's border is inward-only and needs a full-canvas chamfer transform. Flagged, not edited; that document is signed off.
BD-1.3 - the strip field IS ShaperOps.Shell(alignment, width, d), reused verbatim. No second copy of the shell expressions exists; BT-2 checks that bitwise.
BD-2.2 - joining an outward strip to the published coverage is a DILATION (d - reach), NOT min(d, strip). The naive min has a spurious zero crossing exactly on the original silhouette, producing a 1px coverage-0.5 seam following the outline. BT-8 runs the naive form as a control and it genuinely fails at 140-286 samples.
BD-3.5 - a border is NOT in the exclusivity partition; it composites Over its host node's finished subtree. Model (a), making it an owner in the partition, is contradictory in a tree: to sit above a bag's members the bag's border would have to be a descendant of owners that are its own descendants.
BD-3.2 vs BD-3.3 are the deliberate opposite pair: positional inputs normalise on the NODE's anchor box (FC-8.4 verbatim), the edgeDistance sheet is the STRIP's. Both are separately proven and separately proven falsifiable.
