## Description

Grand Unified Generator GUG

In Laubrary i now have Pyre that is an increasing collection of ways to generate pixelart and sprite sheet animations. 

Its clear that its developing into a collection of techniques. 

We call them shapes at the moment but i think they should be reconsidered as generators. A generator is something that can be placed on a Pyre layer and it generates pixelart frames over time. It has its own UI and algorithm to do this. 

Lets call them generators from now untill refactoring. 
Id like to make an indexing over what generators we have and how they differ. Ive gotten some explanations that some have or dont have state and can therefore get different results. There is something called simuation as well. Im not sure if its very useful though. 

We also have the layer matte mask system which seems quite complex. Some generators seem to be able to be part of it and some perhaps cannot? 

We have the spriteFX modifier system and again they work with some generators and others not. 

Some generators create 3d models and use lighting simulations. 



What makes generators work with some spriteFX And what does not? How can we categorise this? How can we make it clear for the user what to expect from a generation in terms of what spriteFXs will actually do anything? 

We have a quite old fuse fuction in the shape view (or was it swarm)? Im pretty sure we will retire that but i want to know what id does as well. 

I also think we have some weird SpriteFXs that use user generated hand drawn paths in the preview window. Im not sure they still work and will probably be culled. 

Have a look at the new web app Shaper3D in the D:Codez/ folder. Its meant to generate pixel art as well. So i am considering to make it a part of Pyre as a generator. But Shaper3d overlaps with some 3d shape generators that already exists. Also shaper3d has its own layersystem which is important. Would a layer with a shaper3D generator then expose its own mode that has a shapes layer which itself can be edited cause it can have multiple internal shapes. or should one pyre layer hold one shapes layer that can be edited with its internal layers?

On top of this, shaper3d has  a crude material system that will be developed more. Ive been thinking about this for pyre. Alot of the generators today work a bit like material generators and shapers at the same time. I mnot sure where to separate this. But it would be cool to have some layers create shapes that may animate over time and then have the surface show a material that can be generated by a generator and also animated. The shape could be a fireball and the material could be churning plasma fire. 

I need a full view of this. I need it explined ELI5 TLDR and then also more in depth. Dont mention any actual code, thats not the point. I am trying to see how we can standardise the system, types of generators, what should be simlplified, concretised, further developed, fused, culled etc...

**Reply** (2026-08-30T18:27:18Z, agent):
Yes. It is possible, you were right to push on it, and it is a good deal cheaper than my earlier answer said.

Why it is possible: the tool already holds everything the harder version needs. Today, for every dot on the canvas it remembers one number - how tall the shape is there - which is why it looks so good from straight above, and also why it cannot turn: tilt a solid and one dot has to answer for both the top and the side, and there is only one slot. The way out is to stop keeping a picture of heights and keep the rule that says whether a point in space is inside the shape or outside it, then for each dot follow a line into the scene until it meets something. Every primitive, every way of combining them, every extrusion profile and every bevel already is that rule. Not one primitive has to be re-expressed and not one dial changes meaning. Turning is then a matter of pointing the line differently.

Where my earlier answer was wrong, plainly: I priced this as throwing away the two hundred best-tuned lines in the app. Those lines belong to the old browser app, in another language, and the rebuild is from scratch - they are being rewritten whichever way you decide, so they were never a cost of rotation at all. I also listed seven things that could not survive a turn, and four of those were wrong.

The measured cost: turning costs roughly seven to eleven times the work per dot of not turning - not the hundreds everyone assumes - and at the ordinary straight-down view it costs nothing extra at all, because the question collapses back into exactly the one the tool asks today. Read seven to eleven as the best case: it was measured on a flat-topped shape, and curved tops cost somewhat more.

What I have done with your ask. The first version still resolves flat, and the ability to turn is designed in from day one rather than left as a hope. Three things are now requirements in the build rather than good intentions: the resolve is specified in its general, line-shaped form with the straight-down version as its first case; surface direction is published by the shape instead of being guessed from the dots around it; and every primitive, and every extrusion profile, declares how far off its own edge-distance can be. That last one is the only piece that is genuinely expensive to add later, so it gets paid now - and it turned up a real trap: one profile family, the stepped one, cannot be bounded at all and needs its own treatment or an honest exclusion. On top of that, one deliberately tilted picture gets rendered and kept as proof the door is really open, because this plan already documents two day-one rules in this codebase that quietly closed.

The honest weakness of that choice, so you can overrule it knowing what it is: a path that runs every frame stays correct because everything depends on it, whereas a kept test picture only catches a break the next time someone looks.

So the one word that is yours. What you have now is the possibility, guaranteed and tested. If you want tilt actually usable in the first version, say so and that becomes the plan. The genuine extra cost is that the spine of the build gets meaningfully longer before Shaper renders anything at all, and the side-wall question - an extruded solid has walls, and a wall has no "how far in from the outline", which is the number the bevels, the borders and the strip fills are all defined against - stops being something to write down and becomes something to answer and build now. Two of the three costs I quoted for this in the first draft were not costs of saying yes at all; I had overpriced your own request by about three times, and that has been corrected.
