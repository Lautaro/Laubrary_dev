## Description

The shape engine. Port 3D Shaper's silhouette mechanism (in the REFERENCE APP its whole primitive registry is about a dozen lines of scalar maths - read that as a description of what exists there, NOT as a promise about what gets built here; the next paragraph explicitly retracts it as a promise, because the Lipschitz-bound requirement ends it and the registry this task delivers is correspondingly larger) and add: angular/linear SWEEP and SHELL as operators applied to the finished shape rather than changes to each primitive's formula, so all primitives gain both at once and identity settings are bit-identical (design B12); each primitive declares whether its sweep runs around it or along its length; a true regular N-gon; and a parameterised star taking Pyre's existing specification (arm count, length, base width, skew, all animatable, with the clamp that stops valleys crossing tips). Take the mechanism from Shaper and the parameters from Pyre - Shaper's hexagon and octagon are hand-fitted axis-aligned approximations and its star is a fixed five-lobe cosine flower with no parameters, so porting them as they stand is a day-one regression.

Each primitive must also declare a Lipschitz bound on the distance it returns, and every transform and combine node must compose those bounds. This is not theory: measured against true Euclidean distance, Shaper's diamond over-reports by 1.414x at a corner, its triangle by 2.236x on a slanted side, its star by up to 3.13x at points inside the shape and 2.39x outside it, and any component carrying a non-uniform scale over-reports by the scale ratio (5.00x measured at scale.x = 0.2, because evalShape uses a child's distance with no rescale by the transform). The star figure is a correction of an earlier draft of this task, which said "1.308x at a lobe tip" and was wrong twice over: re-derived independently by 60,000-direction radial bisection against true Euclidean distance, the lobe tip is the one place on the star where the reported distance is exactly right (1.0000x, because the radial profile's slope vanishes there) - so the tip is precisely where the star is NOT at its worst, and quoting it as the measurement pointed an implementer at the least informative point on the shape. Take the general lesson with the number: a bound must be derived from the worst case over the whole field, inside and outside, never from a sampled point, however natural that point looks. A bound set from a sample is unsafe by whatever the sample missed - here a factor of about 2.4 - and unsafe means a ray striding through thin geometry and leaving holes. None of that matters today, because the current renderer reads only the sign and uses the magnitude as a shaping parameter - it is a sign-plus-shaping field, not a metric field. It matters enormously the moment anything steps along a ray by the returned distance, which is the difference between a rotation feature costing 10x and one costing 400x. The bound is one extra number per compiled node and one divide; it is nearly free here and expensive anywhere else. Note also that subtractSoft is not a distance field at partial strength at all (it adds an authored bite term), so it needs an explicit bound rather than a derived one. This requirement has a second half that is not in this task and is larger than this one: the extrusion and bevel HEIGHT PROFILES are the dominant over-reporters, they are neither primitives nor transforms nor combines so they fall through everything specified here, and they are specified in T-0109. Read the two together - one requirement in two halves - rather than assuming the registry's bounds cover the extruded solid.

The authoritative design is the "Shaper - the design" tab of this group's planning document (also at D:\UNITY\Laubrary Dev\.agenthq\workspace\T-0098\SHAPER_THE_DESIGN.md). Read it before starting. Background: the three earlier reports are tabs of the PyrePlus group's planning document, and a condensed digest of them (incl. every withdrawn claim and every known live bug) is at D:\UNITY\Laubrary Dev\.agenthq\workspace\T-0098\P1-digest.md.

HOW THE BOUNDS WERE ESTABLISHED. Gradient measurement, not ratio sampling: sup|grad f| <= L with f zero on the boundary gives |f| <= L*d over the WHOLE field from a finite sample, which point-sampling the ratio cannot. Use the max directional difference quotient, never hypot of two axis-aligned central differences - the latter has sqrt2 of headroom at a kink and reports 1.02-1.03 on provably exact fields. Below the configured step, subtract a float-rounding bound from the numerator or the instrument manufactures violations (an exact Rect reads 1.037 at h=3e-5).
