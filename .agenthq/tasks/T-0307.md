**What was wrong, and is now right.**

The Canvas card's "Depth between layers" said a layer breaks through "the one below it". A layer's base plane already sits exactly that far above the one below, so it cannot. Compositing is by nearest surface, so the real rule is the opposite: a layer rising past that gap pushes through the layer ABOVE it. On a two-layer document at spacing 4, raising the BOTTOM layer 24 flips the picture from 468 blue pixels to 468 red; raising the TOP layer 24 changes nothing; the same 24 under a spacing of 64 changes nothing. The tooltip now says that. The Layers section header carried the matching overstatement -- "what you see here is the stacking order" -- and now qualifies it, which also stops it contradicting the per-layer Z dial's own tooltip two rows below.

"Document seed" said the seed drives "Min-Max light dials and cherry-frame picks". It is handed to the geometry compile, the fill resolver, the solids, the height stage and the effects pass, so it reaches every Min-Max dial in the document: setting one dial at a time to Min-Max and moving the seed from 1 to 777 moves 8832 pixels on a shape's half-extents, 5716 on a layer's Intensity x, 5536 on a fill's Veil and 3642 on Translate X. An author reading the old sentence would never have looked there.

Twelve tooltips an author hovers were printing internal design-doc codes and source line numbers at them -- "LR-1.3", "B9's authorable range", "(ShaperLightRig.cs:121-131)". All twelve now read as plain sentences and the codes stayed in the comments beside them; 0 of the window's 468 tooltips carries one.
